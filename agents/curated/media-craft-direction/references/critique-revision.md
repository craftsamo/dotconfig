# Critique and revision

Read when evaluating a concept or artifact, choosing a corrective action, or
responding to "flat", "cheap", "stiff", "muddy" or other broad dissatisfaction.

## Separate three questions

1. Does the artifact preserve the requested content, identity and scope?
2. Does its proposed mechanism actually work in the observed media?
3. Does that mechanism help the audience experience the intended result?

Passing one question does not answer the next. A readable paragraph can remain a
weak poster. A visibly connected hinge can remain an unappealing transition. An
unclipped mix can still hide a voice.

Use technical evidence for technical findings and perceptual evidence for
perceptual ones. State image display size and whether a viewer downscaled it.
State the intervals actually sampled in a film; a contact sheet does not prove
normal-speed smoothness. State who listened and what they compared for audio.
An accurate limitation is more useful than a confident invented verdict.

## Diagnose at the right level

Start with the largest mismatch, not the easiest parameter to change. A wrong
concept needs a direction decision. Weak grouping needs composition work. Poor
execution of a good concept may need geometry, timing or sound-envelope changes.
Missing producer capability needs an explicit alternative. Do not send all four
problems into the same "polish" request.

Name evidence, effect and a bounded change: "The supporting labels have the same
weight and occupied area as the headline, so the first reading is ambiguous;
reduce their emphasis while retaining every word." This is more actionable than
"make it premium" and less overconstrained than prescribing every font size.

When a requested change has no observable effect, keep it unmet. Do not accept it
because a source parameter or form string changed. If repeated tuning does not
address the same finding, reconsider the diagnosis before consuming another
authorized attempt. The host, not this skill, decides revision budgets.

## Worked case: texture instead of composition

**Observation.** A public workshop poster has a small centered text block inside
a large plain frame. Grain is added after feedback that the result feels empty.
The next version has the same reading path and little relationship to the topic.

**Diagnosis.** Grain changed surface frequency, not hierarchy or subject
presence. More texture is unlikely to solve the stated emptiness.

**Revision.** Propose a stronger primary element and purposeful grouping: an
original tool silhouette could anchor the workshop identity, with time/place
near the action information. Retain exact copy and brand constraints. If the
client wants a deliberately spare typographic poster, explore type scale and
placement instead of adding an illustration by default.

**Evidence.** Compare at actual display size without the author explaining the
design. Locate the revised primary element and check that secondary information
remains available. A changed background hash is irrelevant to this judgment.

## Worked case: artificial urgency in a welcome bed

**Observation.** A listener reports that a short backing track sounds tense.
The plan called it welcoming; a repeating low pulse and dense bright attacks
occupy the same phrases as the greeting.

**Diagnosis.** The description of the prompt is not evidence against the
listener's experience. Repetition, register and attack density are concrete
hypotheses; without listening, the reviewer must attribute them to the supplied
observations rather than claim to hear them independently.

**Revision.** Within the granted scope, propose fewer simultaneous attacks,
space around the greeting and a less insistent low part. Change a small set of
relevant musical choices, not every parameter. Preserve accepted melody or voice
identity when fixed. If tension is intentional in a different brief, retain it.

**Evidence.** Compare equally contextualized short excerpts; disclose level
differences, source changes and who listened. Do not use a new ASR transcript or
loudness measurement as proof of a friendlier mood.

## Honest outcomes

Use the host's existing outcome vocabulary. Distinguish a diagnostic test with
known defects, a concept ready for client review, a technically valid file and
a final accepted work. A disclosed warning on a test is not automatically a
failed release process, but it is not evidence the aesthetic issue is solved.

Keep subjective disagreement visible. A preference can justify another proposal
without being a universal design fault. Do not average away an explicit missed
requirement using high scores on unrelated dimensions.
