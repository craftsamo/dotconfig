# Reference interpretation

Read when a client supplies media, names an analogy, or asks for something "like
this". A reference is usually evidence of taste, not a complete specification.

## Find the mechanism, not just the nouns

Separate what the example contains from what it does for its audience. A paper
surface might contribute warmth, a page replacement might segment information,
and a drawn character might reduce social distance. All three can occur together;
none follows merely from the word "storybook".

For a visual, trace the first, second and later readings: relative mass, contrast,
placement, grouping, edges and space. Then describe material and motif. For a
sequence, track which object or idea survives between states, what changes, what
causes the change, and where the viewer gets time to understand it. For sound,
separate the foreground event, accompaniment, register, density, pulse, phrase and
ending. Describe actual listening only when it occurred.

Record the scope of every observation. A thumbnail supports overall composition,
not tiny lettering. A still can show a raised lid, not prove the lid opened
naturally. A transcript can establish words, not a reassuring delivery. Metadata
can identify a silent file, not a calm musical mood.

## Translate across contexts

Ask which relationships survive a change of topic, canvas, audience or medium.
An editorial spread's narrow columns may not work in a phone-sized card. The
transferable choice could instead be a clear primary image with compact secondary
captions. A soundtrack's spaciousness may come from few overlapping events, not
from a particular instrument or genre label.

Keep factual copy, logos, product behavior and asset rights out of this inference.
Source media can illustrate a construction without supplying authorized content
for a new work. A technique's feasibility also depends on the chosen producer:
an exact typeset transition and a probabilistically generated shot are different
promises even if their reference looks similar.

## Worked case: "like a field notebook"

**Request.** A community garden wants an inviting sequence explaining how to join;
the client suggests a field-notebook feeling without fixing a literal notebook.

**Weak interpretation.** The proposal specifies rings, worn paper, corner curls
and stitched margins. Joining instructions remain a dense paragraph.

**Diagnosis.** Object recognition has replaced the purpose. Nothing connects the
notebook suggestion to understanding or warmth, and decoration consumes space
without changing the information hierarchy.

**Revision.** Propose short observational panels with one useful sketch and one
action each. A page replacement can separate steps while a recurring location
marker keeps context. Explain that this carries the informal, sequential quality
without requiring stationery mechanics. If the client explicitly wants an actual
notebook, retain it and improve composition within that constraint.

**Evidence.** Compare the first reading at intended display size and one complete
state change. Ask whether the viewer can identify the next action without the
author's explanation. Paper texture alone is not the success criterion.

## Worked case: "warm, not sleepy"

**Request.** A short welcome message needs approachable backing music, inspired
by a supplied clip. A listener describes the clip as warm and lightly buoyant.

**Weak interpretation.** Every part is slow, soft and sustained. The greeting
feels delayed and the bed competes with sentence endings.

**Diagnosis.** "Warm" was translated into a blanket tempo and envelope recipe.
The listener's buoyancy cue was lost. Without listening to the reference, do not
invent its instruments or measured tempo.

**Revision.** Preserve the listener's observation as supplied evidence. Propose
soft timbres with a sparse, lightly articulated pulse and deliberate gaps around
the greeting. Confirm against a short authorized take rather than claiming the
words in the prompt guarantee the mood.

**Evidence.** Human comparison of the same spoken passage with each bed, with
level differences disclosed. Confirm both approachability and forward movement.

## Do not apply mechanically

An exact reconstruction may genuinely require the reference's geometry. A client
can explicitly choose ornamental abundance or literal material simulation. Do
not replace those decisions with minimalism. When the reference is incidental,
do not manufacture a reference-analysis phase.

These are original interpretation heuristics and fictional cases, not measured
claims about a named production system. Reviewed 2026-09-12.
