# Captions, telop and titles vocabulary

A dictionary, not a rulebook. It extends the core `core.md` with words on screen that carry speech and structure: captions and subtitles, Japanese typesetting, film titles, broadcast telop and line layout. Everything is performed by the timeline inside the frame: a hover, click, drag or scroll is a tween the timeline plays, drawn as a cursor, a finger or a moving page, never a real input. The Feel column uses the five feel words defined in `timing.md` "## Feel" (smooth / snappy / soft / heavy / bouncy). For says what an entry is usually applied to; The tell names the detail that makes it read as itself and how it differs from a near neighbour.

## Captions and subtitles

| Name | Feel | For | Looks like | The tell | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pop-on subtitle（ポップオン字幕） | snappy | text | a whole subtitle block appears at once and is replaced by the next at its out time | there is no motion, the block simply appears and disappears, and a small gap between cues makes a swap visible even when the text sits in the same place — vs `live caption block`: whole cues cut on a timecode, not a rolling block | opacity set on at the cue time and off at its end, a small gap between consecutive cues |
| paint-on caption（ペイントオン字幕） | snappy | text | captions accumulate letter after letter in fixed places; whatever has appeared stays still | every glyph's place is set from the outset, so wrapping and centring stay steady during build-up — vs `pop-on subtitle`: builds up instead of a whole cue at once | full line laid out first, per-char visibility set on word times, no reflow |
| roll-up caption（ロールアップ字幕） | snappy | text | live-style captions assemble line by line along the bottom edge, shoving earlier lines upward until they exit the frame | fresh words enter on the lowest row while earlier rows climb a single step, so the eye holds one spot — vs `live caption block`: that one swaps in place with no scroll | line-block y step per arriving line using `power2.out`, a handful of rows, the eldest clipped at the top edge |
| live caption block（生字幕(2行ブロック)） | snappy | text | a pair of lines that the following block supplants in one go, the way Japanese live captioning works | there is no scrolling, so the words hold steady during display and an abrupt swap is the sole cue of progress; each line is a condensed digest of the speech — vs `pop-on subtitle`: a fixed two-line block replaced as a unit | a fixed box of two lines, words added along the timeline, the entire box exchanged at the next cue |
| active-word highlight（現在語ハイライト字幕） | snappy | text | a short group of words is shown per page and just the word being spoken lights up with colour and a faint scale-up | the page itself stays put while the spotlight jumps between words following the audio; the faint scale leaves neighbouring words in place — vs `moving highlight pill`: no box travels here | per-word colour and scale driven by word timestamps, `power2.out`, whitespace left intact |
| moving highlight pill（背景ピル移動） | snappy | text, UI | a rounded coloured box glides behind the spoken word from word to word | the pill stays on screen throughout, so the eye follows the box; the text inside inverts and the box stretches to each word's width | pill x and width tween between word bounds with `power2.out`, text colour set on the active word |
| Hormozi-style pop captions（ショート動画ポップ字幕）, caption spring pop（字幕スプリングポップ） | bouncy/snappy | text | up to three chunky words burst in with a fast rebound, the key word larger, vivid in colour and slanted | a brief overshoot beyond full size that settles back, along with tint and slant on the key word, forms the rhythm; showing a single word per page parks the gaze at the centre | per-word scale `back.out` or a spring with a slight stagger, tint and slant on the active word, a thick outline |
| forced-narrative fade sync（強制字幕のフェード同期） | smooth | text | a translated line for on-screen text fades in and out in time with the original graphic | the subtitle begins roughly midway through the sign's own fade rather than at its start, so both show up and depart together | per-cue opacity on cue times shifted to match the source fade, linear |
| RSVP word flash（RSVP 一語提示） | snappy | text, UI | one word at a time flashes in the same spot, each word placed so its pivot letter lands on one marked point | word length changes but the key letter stays on the mark, so nothing on screen shifts position — vs `active-word highlight`: one word at a time replaces the last, with no page of words | a single span with text set per word time, alignment by the key letter, longer holds on punctuation |
| phrase-aware line break（文節改行字幕） | smooth | text | a subtitle splits between phrases rather than inside one, making every line a self-contained chunk of meaning | every break falls between phrases, so each line is a whole chunk of meaning and none opens with a particle — vs `bunsetsu phrase reveal`: this governs where lines break, that governs how phrases appear | line breaks placed at phrase boundaries computed offline with a phrase segmenter such as BudouX and stored, so the render stays deterministic, each line fading in with `expo.out` |

## Japanese typesetting and writing

| Name | Feel | For | Looks like | The tell | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bunsetsu phrase reveal（文節単位リビール） | smooth | text | Japanese prose shows up chunk after chunk, each piece lifting gently into position from left to right | a single unit, the bunsetsu, drives both the movement and the line breaks, so each piece that arrives is a whole phrase; the rhythm is coarser than a per-character reveal — vs `phrase-aware line break`: that decides where lines break, this decides how phrases appear | text split at phrase boundaries; `Intl.Segmenter('ja')` yields words, not bunsetsu, so the phrase spans are precomputed offline with a phrase segmenter such as BudouX and stored so the render stays deterministic; a unit per stagger step with a small y rise and opacity, `expo.out`, spans decorative over the original sentence |
| punctuation-aware pause（約物ポーズ） | snappy | text, UI | a character reveal holds for a beat after a comma and a longer beat after a full stop or exclamation mark | a closing bracket arrives together with the character before it and the pause comes after the pair; the stops at the marks follow the rhythm of speech — vs `typewriter + caret`: that one keeps a steady rate through the marks | per-char opacity steps with extra gap inserted after punctuation, closing marks attached to the previous char, `steps()` timing |
| tategaki reveal（縦書きリビール） | smooth | text | vertical Japanese shows up glyph by glyph from top to bottom, columns progressing right to left | glyphs fall in from higher up so motion matches the reading direction; at a column's end the reveal hops to the head of the next column leftward, marks occupy the upper right of their cell, a stop or comma that would begin a column hangs beneath, small kana are kept from starting a column, and brackets plus the prolonged-sound mark are rotated a quarter | `writing-mode: vertical-rl`, per-char y and opacity with `expo.out`, two-digit numbers set upright with `text-combine-upright`, a short per-char delay; the per-character split keeps a tate-chu-yoko run and a ruby group as single units |
| ruby sync reveal（ルビ同期リビール） | smooth | text | furigana appear together with their kanji base, the reading never drifting away from it | base and ruby arrive as one unit, the ruby's opacity trailing the base slightly; the reading sits above the base in horizontal text and to the right in vertical | the base and its ruby group move as one unit with `power2.out`; only the `<rt>` element gets an opacity animation, starting a little later than the base, with no transform on it |
| IME kana-kanji conversion（かな漢字変換） | snappy | text, UI | kana are typed in with an underline, then convert into kanji with a candidate list before being committed | three states: composing shows the kana under a thin underline, converting shows the converted text with the active clause under a thick underline or highlight (the text is not bold), committed leaves plain text with the underline gone | typed kana in a span with a thin underline, then the converted text with a thick underline or highlight on the active clause, then plain text with underline removed on commit, `steps()` per keystroke |
| kanji stroke order（漢字書き順） | smooth | text, UI | a kanji is drawn stroke after stroke, following the right sequence and heading, as though inked by hand | each stroke lasts in proportion to its length, so long strokes crawl and dots flick, giving a handwriting rhythm; the sequence follows the textbook order of that character — vs `brush calligraphy write-on`: constant-width strokes, not a tapering brush | stroke paths in order, each a stroke-dashoffset draw with `sine.inOut`, a short gap between strokes, round caps |
| brush calligraphy write-on（筆文字ライトオン） | smooth/heavy | text | characters are inked with a brush, heavy where the tip lands and tapering into a dry sweep | varying width is the giveaway: weight at the landing, a bulge at a stop, then a thin trailing sweep; strokes follow the standard stroke order — vs `kanji stroke order`: here the width varies along each stroke | variable-width stroke shapes under a path mask wider than the strokes, a `sine.inOut` draw per stroke with a short gap |
| manga onomatopoeia impact（擬音インパクト） | heavy/snappy | text, UI | a huge sound word hits the panel, shakes, and leaves concentrated lines radiating to the centre | there is no easing: it lands within a few frames, one flash frame at the stop, then a decaying shake; a thick outline and slant plus focus lines make it a manga panel — vs `emphasis slam`: that is clean type, this is drawn sound lettering with speed lines | scale slam with `none` ease, a seeded decaying shake, radial lines on a separate layer, thick stroke copy, a slant |
| lyric motion tame/tsume（リリックモーション(タメツメ)） | snappy | text | each word hesitates, then lunges to its spot in a single move, in time with the lyric | the curve hardly shifts across its early half and lunges late, arriving on the beat with choppy on-twos movement and a slightly random settle angle | an ease-in-heavy cubic-bezier tween after a hold, stepped at a low frame rate, seeded offset, tint for a couple of frames on landing |
| danmaku comment scroll（弾幕コメント） | smooth | text, UI | viewer comments flow leftward over the picture in many rows | all comments take an equal time to cross, so lengthier ones travel faster; a lane accepts a new comment only after the previous one has progressed some way, and the lettering is white ringed in dark — vs `news ticker crawl`: many independent comments in lanes, not one strip | per-comment lane and x from seeded start times, linear, finite list |
| extra-bold mincho card（極太明朝タイトルカード） | snappy/heavy | text | a title in extra-bold Mincho on a solid black card jumps abruptly between brief cards | movement is absent and each change is a straight cut; the face is squeezed sideways, large and small sizes contrast, and right-angle layouts set the rhythm while only the closing card lingers — vs `fade-through-black card`: cuts, not fades | heavy serif weight, cards switched by opacity set on beats, scale pop on the closing card |
| Japanese subtitle by reading speed（日本語字幕(1秒4文字)） | snappy | text | Japanese subtitles whose screen duration tracks how many characters they carry, the film-subtitle convention | the number of characters determines how long a cue stays; punctuation turns into spaces, half-width for a comma and full-width for a full stop, and the cue cuts in and out with no animation | cue length computed from character count, opacity set at cue times, no easing, line width capped per row |
| vertical subtitle（縦字幕） | snappy | text | a subtitle set vertically at the right side of the frame | the line is a vertical block at the right edge that cuts in and out instead of sliding; the columns read top to bottom | `writing-mode: vertical-rl`, opacity set per cue, two-digit numbers set upright |
| handwritten-style film subtitle（手書き風映画字幕） | soft | text | subtitles in a handwriting-style face cut on and off | every glyph varies slightly in slant, scale and placement, with oversized detached dakuten; the unevenness is fixed and the cue simply cuts | handwriting font, opacity set per cue, fixed per-glyph offsets |

## Titles and film

| Name | Feel | For | Looks like | The tell | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| headline ribbon wrap（見出しリボンラップ） | smooth | text, UI | a headline wraps around like a ribbon, turning away and showing its back before coming round again | the text rides a twisted band, so the letters narrow, flip and reappear along the curve | text on a skewed or curved path, offset tween with `power2.inOut`, the back face shaded darker |
| slow tracking expand（スロー字間拡散） | heavy/smooth | text | for as long as the title stays on screen, its characters drift gradually farther from one another | the title appears motionless, but spacing grows evenly the whole time; viewers register the gap only late, and a quiet tension rises with it — vs `tracking-in`: that one converges and comes to rest, this one keeps opening | letter-spacing tween on `none` ease across a long dwell, with a closing fade |
| fade-through-black card（フェードスルーブラック） | smooth/heavy | text | out of a dark screen a caption card gradually emerges, stays put, and afterwards dissolves back to darkness ahead of the following one | the screen reaches pure black ahead of any lettering, and the dwell is long — vs `flash-frame card cut`: cards fade through black, with no white frame | opacity on a black base up to full and back down using `sine.inOut`, with the next card queued behind the black |
| flash-frame card cut（フラッシュフレームカット） | snappy | text | trailer-style title cards that change on the beat, each change punched by a white frame, so the words seem burned onto the eye | there is no dissolve at all: card A, a white frame, card B; the white is gone almost before it registers and the next card holds still and dark, which is what makes the hit read as impact — vs `hard cut on hit`: that swaps cleanly with no flash between | each card a full-frame layer swapped with `tl.set` on a beat time; a white plate shown for a frame or two at the swap, then cut away; never more than 3 flashes in any one second, and no saturated red full-frame flash |
| perspective opening crawl（オープニングクロール） | smooth/heavy | text | a block of copy lying on a leaning surface climbs away and shrinks into a distant vanishing line, fully disappearing | lines in front look large and swift while those behind look tiny and sluggish; the underlying scroll never changes speed, only depth creates the slowing impression, and a fade covers the final stretch | a plane tilted with rotateX inside a perspective parent, `y` and scale tweened on `none` ease, opacity falling off near the top |
| bass-style racing credits（グラフィック線のクレジット） | snappy | text | credits glide in beside thick graphic rules that shoot across the screen, then settle into a grid | a rule shoots ahead, and the lettering rides along it at an unchanging pace before locking in; a slanted grid supplies the architectural feel | line drawing with staggered text on linear motion, a skewed grid layer, and exits heading in opposing directions |
| sliced bars title（横線分断タイトル） | snappy | text, logo | the title is chopped into horizontal bands, which enter from left and right in turn, join up into the full title, then scatter | bands tumble in from the top, each led by a slim coloured bar, and the lettering takes shape behind that bar | clipped bands with mirrored `x` offsets, a tight stagger, `expo.out` |
| scratched film jitter（フィルム傷とジッター） | snappy/heavy | text | scratches and a shaky picture overlay the title, along with a faint ghost image | the shake advances in discrete frames with hard edges instead of gliding, scratch marks pass through the letterforms, and the intro feels cut into brief shots | seeded scratch lines, small `x`/`y` hops on `steps()`, an offset duplicate at low opacity |
| rubber-stamp cut-out pop（スタンプ切り絵ポップ） | bouncy/snappy | text | flat, stamped-looking glyphs and shapes jump in, locked to the rhythm in stuttering increments, as the backdrop colour flips | all motion runs at a reduced frame rate, every word overshoots briefly on each beat, and the backdrop cuts hard at every hit | scale pop on `steps()`, rotate, a rough knock-out mask, backdrop colour assigned per beat |
| light-leak title burn（ライトリーク焼き込み） | soft/smooth | text | a warm wash of light floats across the title and grows until it blows out to white for the cut | the light layers additively and brightens only the dark regions; once it peaks the screen is blown out, the title appears inside that glare, and the glow then fades away | a blurred warm gradient layer using `mix-blend-mode: screen`, opacity swelling with `sine.inOut` |
| letterbox reveal（レターボックス開き） | smooth/heavy | text | dark bars along the upper and lower edges slide open and uncover a title card in a widescreen cinema window | the picture opens dark, the bars retreat evenly and halt at a wide slot, and the lettering shows up just as they come to rest, as if the bars pushed it into view | a pair of bars tweened on `y` with `power2.inOut`, text fading in slightly afterwards |

## Broadcast and telop

| Name | Feel | For | Looks like | The tell | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lower third（ロワーサード） | snappy/smooth | text, UI | a name strap rises into the lower part of the frame, holds long enough for a glance, then leaves; a short name above a smaller role line is the usual content | the strap assembles in stages: a thin accent rule first, then the name plate, then the role plate a beat later, each plate arriving before its words; once built, nothing moves at all — vs `side super`: that one lives in a corner and swaps a label | three layers on offset starts, each plate wiping in on `expo.out` with its text masked within; the exit plays the stages backwards, quicker |
| news ticker crawl（ニュースティッカー） | smooth/snappy | text, UI | a strip of headlines glides horizontally at an even pace along a lower banner | each frame moves by a whole number of pixels, and the row is cloned so the loop closes without a visible join — vs `end credits roll`: sideways along a banner, not upward | a cloned row with `x` advanced linearly in integer pixel increments, wrapping at the width of one copy |
| breaking news banner（速報バナー） | snappy | text, UI | a weighty banner sweeps across with a flash of colour, and the headline crashes onto it | the banner stretches to full width first and blinks in colour several times; the headline arrives some frames afterwards with a hard skew, and everything then sits still | bar `scaleX` with `power2.out`, colour swapping on the bar alone for several beats, label pop afterwards, then a hold |
| end credits roll（エンドクレジット） | smooth | text | credits travel upward from the lower edge at an unchanging pace, with roles in small type and names in large | each frame shifts by an identical integer pixel count, so nothing stutters; whites are toned down and blacks raised so fine strokes stay steady | a single tall column with `y` moved linearly in integer pixel steps per frame, overall duration set by the column's height |
| LED dot-matrix marquee（LED 電光掲示板） | snappy | text, UI | lettering built from lit dots hops horizontally over a lattice of glowing points | the message advances by a single dot column per tick on a regular beat, and dim unlit dots convince the eye it is a real board; enough rows keep kanji readable | a dot lattice with `x` moved by `steps()` per column, unlit dots drawn at low opacity, plus a glow duplicate |
| pop-in double-outline telop（二重フチのポン出しテロップ） | bouncy/snappy | text, UI | a bold gothic-style caption edged in white inside and colour outside bursts in slightly oversize and then relaxes to its size | the pop lasts only a handful of frames, a quick rise followed by a briefer return, commonly paired with a sound effect; the white inner line, coloured outer line and soft drop shadow separate it from the footage — vs `comic pop`: no tilt or hard offset shadow, it is the TV-variety outline | scale from zero past full size and back with `back.out`, three stacked stroke layers (inner white, outer colour, shadow) beneath the fill |
| shaking telop（揺れテロップ） | snappy | text, UI | a caption quivers with tiny fast twitches, suggesting dread, chill, rage or thrill | every character shifts a bit differently from the ones beside it, so the effect looks like separate glyphs shaking instead of one slab; the thin outline marks where it rests — vs `wiggly jitter`: a broadcast caption with an outline, tremor throughout the line's stay | seeded per-char `x`/`y`/rotate offsets on `steps()`, applied just for the duration of the line, then easing back to rest |
| zabuton plate wipe（座布団ワイプテロップ） | snappy/smooth | text, UI | a coloured backing plate, the zabuton, sweeps in ahead and the caption glides on top | the plate sweeps from the left edge, the lettering trails by some frames, and the exit runs backwards: lettering leaves first, then the plate sweeps off rightward; plate colour follows a programme convention such as segment or speaker | plate `clip-path` or `scaleX` from the left with `expo.out`, text layer above it starting later, exit in reverse order |
| tsukkomi slam telop（ツッコミテロップ） | snappy/bouncy | text, UI | a comeback line crashes in on a sharp diagonal while the screen rattles and radial speed lines erupt | it lands within frames from an oversized, tilted state, the whole picture rattles briefly, and the lines fade fast, leaving the slanted lettering — vs `emphasis slam`: tilted, with speed lines, the variety-show comeback | scale slam using `power2.in` with a small bounce, slight tilt, a seeded shake on the wrapper, speed lines in their own layer |
| sparkle-appear telop（キラキラ出現テロップ） | bouncy/soft | text, UI | a caption springs into view as glimmers and star shapes scatter outward around it | the stars scatter and fade fast, then after a short pause a sheen of light passes over the glyph interiors alone — vs `sparkles text`: a one-time burst around a caption, not a continuing twinkle | scale pop with `back.out`, a seeded sparkle layer around the text, a masked light sweep across the glyphs |
| comment-follow telop（コメントフォロー(話者色)） | bouncy/snappy | text | brief paraphrases of each performer's remarks show up one after another near the lower edge, every one tinted with that person's own colour | typeface and placement are fixed, and only the outline hue reveals the talker; the talker's colour switches on, the caption pops in, and it lingers for a normal Japanese subtitle reading pace | outline colour assigned per speaker, `back.out` pop, a short wipe-in, hold timed to character count, summary text per line |
| side super（サイドスーパー） | smooth | text, UI | a vertical caption sits at the frame edge, the show name fixed at one corner and the segment name at the other | the show name stays still, only the segment name swaps, the old one up and the new from below; nothing else moves — vs `lower third`: always present and quiet | `writing-mode: vertical-rl` strip or corner labels, a short slide-and-fade on the segment swap with `power2.inOut`, held for the segment |

## Layout and reflow

| Name | Feel | For | Looks like | The tell | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| line break reflow（改行リフロー） | heavy | text, UI | a paragraph rewraps as its box narrows, words sliding to new line positions with a firm settle | each word glides from where it was to where it lands, and the lines carry weight as they shift rather than snapping | container width tween with FLIP on words, `expo.out` |
| counterweight headline（カウンターウェイト見出し） | smooth | text, UI | like the two pans of a scale, one line slides out to the left while its partner glides in from the right and they meet in a tidy stack | the two paths are mirror images in distance and timing, both slow at the ends and quick in the middle, and they finish on the same frame, so the pair is symmetric — vs `alternating list`: that one staggers rows in turn, this one moves two lines as a single counterpoise | two line layers, one offset left and one right of the final column, tweened toward it on `power2.inOut` with the same start; a mask clips each at the frame edge |
| fit-to-width title（幅ぴったり見出し） | snappy | text, logo | every line is enlarged or reduced until it spans the same width, edge to edge | the available width, rather than the letter tally, decides the size, so a brief line ends up bigger — vs `stacked justified words`: that assembles a block with a word on each row | per-line scale derived from measured ink width with `power2.inOut`, measured once the font has loaded |
| stacked justified words（均等幅スタック） | snappy/heavy | text | words of unequal size are each fitted to a single width and pile into a solid rectangle | brief words swell and lengthy ones shrink so all edges meet shared guides, while every incoming word glides up from underneath and lifts the pile | per-row font size taken from measured ink bounds, each row sliding up from beneath with `power2.out` |
| layout-animated word swap（レイアウト追従ワード入替） | smooth/soft | text, UI | when one word is swapped for another of a different length the neighbouring words slide to the new width | the word swaps vertically inside its slot while the surrounding words and container spring into place together | FLIP on siblings with a soft spring-like ease, the old word exiting upward and the new one entering from below |
| anagram FLIP（アナグラム並び替え） | smooth | text, logo | letters physically move across to fresh positions and spell a different word built from the same letters | no letter fades out; each one follows an arc to its new slot, right-bound letters passing over and left-bound ones passing under, so none collide | FLIP per letter between two layouts along arcs, `power3.out`, a slight stagger |
| balanced wrap reflow（バランス改行と再分割） | smooth | text, UI | line breaks rebalance and the lines even out when the width changes | line lengths remain close to equal at every width, and when resizing halts the text splits afresh and the mask reveal plays again | width tween with balanced wrapping, FLIP on words, `expo.out` |
| line-spacing collapse（行間の収束） | smooth/heavy | text | a loose, airy block of lines squeezes together like a spring being compressed, until it reads as one dense paragraph | the letters keep their size and spacing throughout; only the gaps between lines close, with the slowest part at the start and the end, and the last baselines almost touch before the block holds still — vs `soft shrink`: that scales everything down, this one changes only the vertical gap | one text block whose line-height tweens from open to tight on `power2.inOut`, anchored at its first line so the head stays put; ends on a still hold |
| kinetic camera follow（カメラ追従キネティック） | snappy/heavy | text | a camera sweeps from phrase to phrase over a canvas, rotating so each one sits upright | the words never move; on reaching a phrase turned a quarter, the camera rotates to match, and a final zoom-out exposes the full composition | a camera wrapper with position, rotation and scale keyed to phrase positions, a strong ease-in-out, a brief hold on each arrival |
| truncate expand（省略から全文展開） | smooth | text, UI | a line that ends in an ellipsis opens up to its full length, the box extending downward | the ellipsis vanishes, extra lines slide down from above beneath a travelling mask, and everything underneath is shoved down in step | height tween with the ellipsis swapped out, FLIP on the content below |
