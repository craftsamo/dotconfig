# Timing, easing and feel vocabulary

A dictionary, not a rulebook: names for how a motion moves, namely principles, easing curves, springs and rhythm. Its "## Feel" table defines the five feel words every Feel column in this vocabulary uses. The tell names the detail that makes an entry read as itself and how it differs from a near neighbour. How each entry is built in a rendered video is in [video/timing.md](video/timing.md).

## Feel

Every motion table in the vocabulary carries a Feel column with these words, so an entry can be found from a feel the user describes.

| Feel | Japanese | Reads as | Usually comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| smooth | なめらか | continuous, unbroken travel with no visible start or stop | long soft landings, ease-out expo and quint, sine in-out, critically damped spring, linear for constant travel |
| snappy | きびきび | quick, decisive, the move is over almost as it starts | very fast ease-out, circ out, steps, short hold after the arrival, cuts on the beat grid |
| soft | やわらかい | gentle and cushioned, edges rounded off | slow sine in-out, squashy settle, loose spring with low stiffness, random stagger |
| heavy | 重い | a big mass that is slow to start and slow to stop | longer duration for larger objects, strongly damped slow spring, speed ramp into a hit |
| bouncy | 弾む | playful rebound, the object overshoots or hops back | back out, elastic out, bounce out, springs with low damping, squash and stretch |

## Principles

| Name | Feel | Looks like | The tell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| staging（ステージング） | smooth | one subject steps forward while everything around it recedes, so the eye has a single place to go | the surroundings dim and the hero grows a touch, finishing together — vs `primary / secondary / ambient`: that one layers speeds, this one narrows the focus |
| pose to pose / straight ahead（ポーズ・トゥ・ポーズ、ストレートアヘッド） | smooth | two ways of building one movement: key poses with tidy in-betweens, or a motion grown frame by frame from the previous frame | the first lands exactly where designed with an even path; the second wanders and may miss its end point, like fire and particles — vs `slow in and slow out`: that one is about the speed curve between poses |
| slow in and slow out（スローイン、スローアウト） | smooth | a move that leaves rest gently and comes to rest gently, with the speed bunched in the middle | against a constant-speed copy the difference is obvious: the plain one starts and stops abruptly, the eased one crowds its spacing at both ends — vs `linear`: no ramp at either end |
| arcs（アーク、弧の軌道） | smooth | a limb or object travels along a curve instead of a straight line between two points | the path bows sideways through a midpoint pushed off the straight line; the bow is a shallow sideways dip off the straight line — vs `projectile`: that arc is gravity, this one is a chosen bow |
| secondary action（セカンダリーアクション） | soft/smooth | a small side motion supports the main one, such as a blink beside a nod | the extra move is clearly smaller than the hero, starts a little later and uses a different ease, so the lead stays obvious — vs `follow-through / lag`: that one trails the same body, this adds a separate gesture |
| exaggeration（誇張） | bouncy | a move pushed past plausible so the idea is unmistakable, such as a success check swelling beyond its size before settling | the overshoot is modest but clear and the return is short — vs `back out`: that is the curve, this is the decision to spend it |
| solid drawing / depth（ソリッドドローイング、立体感） | smooth | a card or form turns in space and reads as an object with thickness, not a flat sheet | its shadow shifts and stretches the opposite way as the tilt changes, with perspective set on the parent — vs `arcs`: that one bends a path, this one gives a body volume |
| appeal（アピール、気持ちよさ） | snappy/smooth | a control that feels pleasing and clear at a glance, such as a toggle knob that moves and lets its label follow | the knob stretches only while it travels, then returns to its true shape, and the colour changes a beat after the knob starts; the flourish is quieter than the feedback itself — vs `squash and stretch`: that one deforms a whole body on impact, this one stretches a control only while it travels |
| cause-and-effect chain（原因と結果の連鎖） | smooth | one action visibly triggers the next, like dominoes, each piece answering the last | each tween starts at the contact point of the previous one with a gap of only a few frames — vs `stagger: sequential`: that one runs on a regular clock, this one on contact |
| anti-robotic jitter（アンチ・ロボット、ゆらぎ） | soft/smooth | a row of siblings that arrive in a nearly even rhythm but with tiny differences, so nothing looks machined | the unevenness is just under the eye's threshold: timing and size vary by a hair per sibling and repeat identically on every render — vs `stagger: random`: that scatters the order, this keeps the order and blurs the spacing |
| primary / secondary / ambient（主役・脇役・背景の三層） | smooth | a lead motion, a smaller supporting one and a slow background drift share the frame | three speeds are visible at once: the hero big and early, the secondaries smaller and later, the background always drifting — vs `staging`: that one dims the rest, this one keeps it moving at a lower rank |
| no dead frames（止まって見えるコマを作らない） | smooth/soft | during a reading pause something still moves a little, so the picture does not look frozen | the moving thing is the background or a single element, with small amplitude and a long period — vs `reading hold`: that is the still pause itself, this keeps it alive |
| counter-motion（カウンターモーション） | snappy | one part moves against another, which sharpens both, such as the hero entering one way while the background slides the other | the background's counter-move is much slower than the hero's and shares its duration and ease, while the ground drifts against the hero — vs `parallax layers`: those move the same way at different rates, this opposes |

## Easing

| Name | Feel | Looks like | The tell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| linear（linear、等速） | smooth | travel at one constant speed from start to end, like a clock hand | the speed stays flat, so the start and the stop are abrupt, as on spins and drifts that have no beginning or end — vs `slow in and slow out`: that one eases both ends |
| ease-out expo（入り） | snappy/smooth | a fast launch that coasts to a very soft stop, the usual way in | nearly all the distance is covered in the first few frames and the rest is a long tail, like a flick of a finger on a smooth surface; no overshoot — vs `ease-out quint`: this launches harder and its tail is longer and quieter |
| ease-in expo（退場） | snappy | a slow start that accelerates away, the usual way out | it is almost motionless at first and fastest at the very end, as if something lets go and drops away; there is no braking phase — vs `Material 3 accelerate`: this one gathers speed more abruptly |
| ease-in-out cubic（移動） | smooth | a move between two places with an even ramp up and down | the speed is mirrored: slow, fast in the middle, slow, with equal time spent leaving and arriving — vs `thin-peak curve`: that one keeps its ends nearly still and bursts in the middle |
| thin-peak curve（シンピーク、ハウスカーブ） | smooth/snappy | a very narrow burst of speed in the middle with long calm ends | the ends are almost still, then the distance is crossed in a short rush in the middle and lands softly — vs `ease-in-out cubic`: this has far calmer ends and a sharper middle |
| whip curve（WHIP、ムチ） | snappy | almost no motion, then a lash of speed and a hard brake, like a cracked whip | nearly all the distance is covered within a frame or so mid-way, so the middle of the path stays blank — vs `thin-peak curve`: this middle is almost vertical and the stops are harder |
| glide（グライド） | smooth | a travel that eases in gently and floats to rest over a long tail | speed rises softly and the deceleration is long and quiet, the stop is hardly noticeable — vs `ease-out expo`: that one launches instantly, this one builds first |
| ease-out quint（ease-out quint） | smooth | a quick start with a long, gradual settle | the braking is softer than expo, which keeps movement perceptible right until it stops even over a long run — vs `ease-out expo`: a softer launch with a more readable tail |
| Material 3 standard（Material 3 標準） | smooth/snappy | a brisk start that eases into the end, used for on-screen moves | the first part is quick and the second half gently settles onto the target, with no overshoot — vs `Material 3 emphasized`: that one leaps harder and decelerates longer |
| Material 3 emphasized（Material 3 強調、減速） | smooth/snappy | a very quick launch and a long deceleration, the most expressive platform curve | it surges ahead during the opening frames, then slows over a prolonged stretch into a dramatic halt, like a heavy door closing on a damper — vs `Material 3 standard`: far more front-loaded |
| Material 3 accelerate（Material 3 加速、退場） | snappy | starts slow and speeds up as the element leaves | it has no braking phase: the speed keeps rising until the last frame before it vanishes, usually with a fade — vs `ease-in expo`: a gentler build with a lower final speed |
| Apple standard（Apple 標準） | smooth | an even, gentle ease that starts and ends softly | the curve is front-loaded: a short rise and a longer deceleration, quiet and unremarkable — vs `sine in-out`: that one is symmetric and this leans forward |
| back out（オーバーシュート） | bouncy | goes past the target, then eases back to it | one overshoot only: the object crosses the final position by a small amount and returns — vs `elastic out`: that wobbles several times, this swings past once |
| back in（引いてから去る） | bouncy/snappy | pulls back a little, then leaves quickly | the first part of the curve dips backwards before it launches forward, like a wind-up before a throw — vs `ease-in expo`: this one recoils first |
| back in-out（back in-out） | bouncy | pulls back, rushes across, overshoots, and settles | recoil at both ends: it dips behind the start, crosses at speed, then goes past the target once before landing — vs `back out`: that has only the overshoot end |
| sine in-out（呼吸） | smooth/soft | a slow, even swell like breathing | speed changes so softly that the reversal points of an oscillating loop go unnoticed — vs `linear`: that one turns around with a visible jolt |
| circ out（circ out） | snappy | a hard, fast start that brakes sharply at the end | velocity stays high through most of the run, then drops steeply along a rounded curve into the stop, producing a mechanical, gadget-like click — vs `ease-out expo`: this holds its speed longer and then stops harder |
| elastic out（ゴム） | bouncy | rubber-band wobble around the target before it rests | it swings through the target several times with shrinking amplitude, like a stretched band let go, and the rest arrives only after the last small swing — vs `bounce out`: that one rebounds off a floor and stays on one side of the target |
| bounce out（弾む） | bouncy | falls to the target and hops back up in smaller bounces | it behaves like a ball on the floor: each rebound is lower and quicker, ending in a rest, like a drop or a landing — vs `elastic out`: this bounces off the end value without passing through it |
| steps / posterize（steps、コマ打ち） | snappy | motion jumps between a few fixed positions with no in-between | the object hops from one position to the next on a regular clock, like a clock hand or a flip book, and the coarser the clock the more mechanical the motion — vs `linear`: the same pace, but cut into discrete jumps |
| SlowMo（速い→遅い→速い） | smooth | fast, then a long slow middle, then fast again | it enters fast, nearly hangs in the middle where the key moment sits, then speeds out — vs `thin-peak curve`: that one is slow at the ends and fast in the middle, the reverse |
| RoughEase（ガタつき） | snappy | the travel is jittery and uneven on its way to the target | progress is made overall, but the value jitters back and forth along the way — vs `CustomWiggle`: that one is a clean decaying swing, this one is irregular noise |
| ExpoScale（等速に見えるズーム） | smooth | a zoom that reads as constant speed, not slowing as it nears | a plain linear scale seems slow at the start and rushing at the end, while this one keeps the apparent rate even across the whole zoom; over a small range the two look alike — vs `linear`: linear in scale feels uneven, this is linear in perception |
| CustomWiggle（揺れ） | bouncy/snappy | a shake that swings back and forth and dies out | it oscillates a fixed number of times around the value, each swing smaller, then returns to rest, like a bell or a head-shake — vs `elastic out`: that one starts from a move, this one shakes in place |
| CustomBounce with squash（CustomBounce＋スクワッシュ） | bouncy | a ball-like object pancakes briefly on every touchdown | the flattening lasts just while it touches the ground and the shape recovers in the air, each rebound climbing lower with a milder flatten; in the air the shape is whole — vs `bounce out`: that curve moves position alone, this layers shape change on top |

## Springs

| Name | Feel | Looks like | The tell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| snap spring（指ではじく） | snappy | a stiff, tight flick that lands immediately with hardly any wobble | velocity peaks right at the start and motion halts nearly at once, at most a single slight overshoot, like flicking a lightweight object — vs `bounce spring`: no repeated rebounds |
| bounce spring（ボール） | bouncy | like a ball, a clear overshoot and several shrinking rebounds | it reaches the target quickly then overshoots clearly and returns a few times, each rebound smaller — vs `elastic out`: this is a physical spring with a visible rest |
| smooth spring（ダンパー付きの扉） | smooth | a door with a damper: it glides and closes without a bounce | the speed rises, then eases off into rest with no overshoot at all; it carries velocity through an interruption, which is what separates it from a plain ease — vs `critically damped`: that one is the fastest limit, this one is somewhat slower and calmer |
| heavy spring（重い物） | heavy | a weighty object that is slow to start and settles slowly | it begins slowly, may overshoot a little, and takes its own time to rest, like a large panel on a spring — vs `snap spring`: the opposite response |
| react-spring default（react-spring default） | smooth | the library's everyday motion, a soft move with a tiny overshoot | it arrives with almost no overshoot and a neutral pace — vs `smooth spring`: a touch quicker and plainer |
| react-spring wobbly（react-spring wobbly） | bouncy/soft | a loose, jelly-like swing that takes a while to rest | it swings several times around the target, each swing smaller and slower, like jelly on a plate — vs `bounce spring`: this is looser and softer, less ball-like |
| react-spring molasses（react-spring molasses） | heavy/smooth | syrup-like and sluggish, creeping into place | a strong pull meets strong resistance, so the element oozes to rest over a long span without overshooting — vs `heavy spring`: this never overshoots and creeps far slower |
| Framer bouncy（Framer bouncy） | bouncy/snappy | a quick spring with a lively overshoot | it hits the target almost immediately, overshoots clearly once or twice and snaps back — vs `bounce spring`: quicker and tighter, fewer rebounds |
| Apple modal（Apple モーダル） | smooth/snappy | a sheet rises quickly and settles with barely any overshoot | it rises fast and overshoots by a hair, settling quietly; closing it uses a short ease-in — vs `critically damped`: this one has a trace of overshoot |
| critically damped（臨界減衰、揺れない最速） | smooth/snappy | the fastest arrival that does not swing past the target | the speed drops straight to rest the moment it reaches the target, with no overshoot; slightly less damping would wobble and more would slow it — vs `smooth spring`: faster and sharper at the end |
| summed springs（目標が変わるバネ、足し合わせ） | smooth | when the target changes mid-move, the motion bends toward the new one without a stop | the speed is kept through each change instead of restarting from rest, so the path curves smoothly toward the new target — vs `spring vs tween`: that one compares the two, this one is the spring way of retargeting |
| split-edge springs（伸びるタブ、前後で別のバネ） | soft/smooth | a tab elongates because its front edge dashes forward while the back edge follows later | the front edge lands first and the back edge trails, so the bar is stretched mid-travel and regains its normal width at rest, liquid-like; the two edges follow different springs — vs `follow-through / lag`: this deforms one shape, that one drags separate pieces behind |
| spring vs tween（スプリング vs トゥイーン） | smooth | a spring carries speed through a change, a tween restarts from a stop | the object on a spring lands with momentum, while a tween stops exactly on schedule — vs `summed springs`: that one is how a spring retargets, this is the choice between the two |
| overshoot budget（オーバーシュートの予算） | bouncy/smooth | the amount of overshoot is spent on a few lead items and the rest settle plainly | similar moves differ only in how far they pass the target, stepping from none to a clear amount; some moments get none and others the most — vs `back out`: that is one curve, this is how its strength is distributed |
| inertia / momentum（慣性、投げて止まる） | smooth | a flicked object glides and halts through its own friction | it departs at the release velocity and decelerates evenly like moving over a rough surface, with the final position optionally quantised to a grid; the travel length follows the release velocity — vs `projectile`: that arc comes from gravity, this from friction |
| projectile（放物運動、Physics2D） | bouncy | a thrown thing traces a falling arc as gravity curves its path | sideways speed stays constant while upward speed fades until the apex and then grows on the descent, like a tossed ball — vs `arcs`: that arc is a chosen curve, this one is physics |

## Timing and rhythm

| Name | Feel | Looks like | The tell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| duration by size（大きさで尺を変える） | heavy/snappy | big things take longer to move and small things move quickly | launched on the same beat, a chip, a dialog and a full-width banner land one after another from smallest to largest, so the duration itself reads as weight — vs `distance-scaled duration`: that one scales with travel, this with area |
| distance-scaled duration（距離で尺を変える、√ の法則） | smooth | farther trips last longer, though not proportionally | doubling-style growth in time lags behind growth in distance, so far and near moves feel similarly brisk — vs `duration by size`: this one follows distance, that one follows size |
| exit faster than enter（退場は登場より短く） | snappy | things arrive at ease and leave quickly | the entrance settles slowly while the exit accelerates away in clearly less time, so the exit is shorter than the entrance — vs `ease-in expo`: that is the curve, this is the length relation |
| stagger: sequential（ステッガー：順番に） | smooth | items show up one by one in sequence | together they form a single flow in reading order from the first item, with a small gap between items — vs `cause-and-effect chain`: this is a regular clock, that one is contact |
| stagger: from center（ステッガー：中央から） | smooth | arrival spreads outward from the middle | the middle item shows first and each ring after it, like ripples on a pond — vs `stagger: from edges`: the same ripple, reversed |
| stagger: from edges（ステッガー：両端から） | smooth | arrival gathers inward from both flanks | items at either end show first and the middle is filled last, so the run closes like a convergence — vs `stagger: from center`: it runs inward, that runs outward |
| stagger: random（ステッガー：ランダム） | soft | items arrive in a scattered order | the order has no pattern and the whole adds up to a sparkling texture — vs `anti-robotic jitter`: that keeps the order, this scrambles it |
| stagger: grid diagonal（ステッガー：グリッド斜め） | smooth | a grid fills in along a diagonal wave | a band of arrival runs from one corner across to the opposite one, each cell starting by its row plus column — vs `stagger: sequential`: that follows a line, this follows a plane |
| snap, then hold（パッと出て、止めて見せる） | snappy | a fast arrival, then a still pause that lets it be read | the change itself takes only a frame or two and only the small settle after it eases; the pause that follows is long enough to register — vs `reading hold`: that one is sized by the text, this one is the contrast after a snap |
| beat grid（拍のグリッド） | snappy | cuts and hits fall on the regular pulse of the music | minor changes follow each beat while the major move takes the bar's opening beat only — vs `energy arc`: this is the micro clock, that the overall shape |
| speed ramp into a hit（スピードランプ） | heavy/snappy | motion accelerates, then slams into a stop that feels heavy | just before the impact the speed falls sharply to build tension, then snaps back to full speed exactly at the hit, the slow stretch being only a breath long — vs `snap, then hold`: this is time bending before the hit, that is a pause after it |
| overlap levels（重なり、オーバーラップの量） | smooth/snappy | the following move begins before the previous one finishes, slightly or heavily | the degree of overlap sets the pacing: none looks deliberate, a little crisp, half flowing, and with more the moves appear to happen simultaneously — vs `stagger: sequential`: this is the overlap fraction, that the fixed gap |
| reading hold（読める時間を確保する） | smooth | text or a figure stays still long enough to read | once the text appears nothing moves for a time that grows with its length, and even a short phrase gets more than a moment — vs `no dead frames`: that one keeps something drifting, this one is fully still |
| energy arc（エネルギーの弧） | smooth | pace rises toward a peak and then calms, across a whole video | the amount of motion starts quiet, builds, peaks late and resolves briefly — vs `beat grid`: that one is the rhythm inside, this is the shape of the whole |
