# Media Craft Direction

Use this knowledge at a decision, not as another production pipeline. The host
owns intake, production, budgets, approval, storage and delivery. Keep its existing
records; this skill requires no new brief document, scoring service or agent.
Explicit client constraints and the producer's actual operating contract win over
a craft suggestion. A conflict is a proposal to resolve, not permission to relax
the constraint. Never infer upload, generation or publication consent from a
reference.

## Read for the decision

- When a reference or analogy drives the brief, read
  [Reference interpretation](references/reference-interpretation.md).
- Before choosing a concept or declaring something immutable, read
  [Direction and constraints](references/direction-constraints.md).
- When a producer needs actionable direction, or parts must form a whole, read
  [Production translation](references/production-translation.md).
- When judging a result or responding to dissatisfaction, read
  [Critique and revision](references/critique-revision.md).

Read only the material needed for the current decision. A known, successful look
does not need another concept round. If the required reference cannot actually be
seen or heard, retain that gap; ask for the missing evidence only when the choice
depends on it. A textual description is evidence of what someone described, not
direct observation of the media.

## The useful output

State the intended audience effect, the visible or audible mechanism proposed to
produce it, the conditions that must survive, and the evidence that would settle
the uncertain part. These may be a few sentences in the host's existing brief or
review. Do not make the client fill in a technique sheet.

Keep four different things distinct:

- **Observed:** what an inspected frame, sequence or listening report supports.
- **Suggested:** a client's tentative idea or the producer's interpretation.
- **Decided:** what the client actually fixed, with its scope intact.
- **Open:** a choice still affecting the deliverable.

An implementation choice can be made within delegated discretion. It does not
become a new client requirement merely because it was written into a proposal.
Do not ask for a taste vote on every minor choice; ask when a decision changes
purpose, protected content, expenditure, or a materially different direction.

## Know what this does not prove

Well-described craft is not demonstrated craft. A matching form, readable text,
valid render, exact hash or correctly timed cue does not establish appeal. Review
the actual deliverable when available, distinguish sampled from continuous
evidence, and carry uncertainty through the final handoff. Prefer a concrete
finding over a universal aesthetic score.

Other craft knowledge can inform implementation, but this skill neither selects
an engine nor supplies its API. Use only controls the actual producer supports.
When no change is needed, explain why the existing choice serves the purpose and
leave it alone.
