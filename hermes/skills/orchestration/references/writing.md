# Writing capability — plan / execute / qa

Load when the deliverable is text: reader-facing prose (marketing copy,
articles, documentation) or producer-facing scripts (漫画台本, 絵コンテ,
storyboards, screenplays consumed by creator or artists). The specialist is
the **writer** resident session — it drafts; it never publishes.

## Plan

Settle the WritingBrief before the session, from chat and memory:

- **Type** — copy / article / documentation / script.
- **Audience & purpose** — who reads it, what it must achieve.
- **Medium & tone** — where it appears; register (敬体/常体), voice, any
  tone samples you can paste.
- **Length / budget** and **language**.
- **Sources** — links, research conclusions (paste them — see
  `references/research.md`), product facts.
- For production scripts: the artifact file name and the unit/field
  conventions the downstream producer expects (panels, cuts, timing).

The writer routes prose-vs-script and its Japanese norms layers itself —
brief the WHAT, not the style rules. Missing premises (tone samples, target
length) surface as one question round in its first reply; answer or relay.

## Execute

Start the session with the brief. Supervise:

- The deliverable is a file at a durable path (default `deliverable.md`
  under `~/Workspaces/.deliverables/<job>/`) plus a reply summarizing
  structure and choices.
- Feedback turns are itemized and quote-anchored ("リード文が硬い — 例:
  …", "第2章は結論を先に"). The session holds the draft; revisions are
  cheap.
- Text consumed by creator (scripts, captions) flows: writer turn → your
  QA → paste/attach into the creator session's brief.

## QA

- Read the actual file, whole — never judge from the reply summary.
- Check against the brief: type, audience fit, tone, length, language,
  required facts present and correct (spot-check claims against the pasted
  sources).
- Japanese deliverables: notation consistency (表記ゆれ), register
  stability, no LLM-smell filler; scripts additionally: the downstream
  producer's field/unit conventions are honored exactly.
- Defects go back as an itemized feedback turn.

## Pitfalls

- Publishing writer output anywhere — publishing is marketer's, behind a
  Publish approval.
- Briefing style mechanics the writer's own norms layers already own.
- Judging a draft by its summary, or forwarding unread text.
- Splitting one document across writer and your own inline edits — one
  owner per document; give feedback instead of patching.
