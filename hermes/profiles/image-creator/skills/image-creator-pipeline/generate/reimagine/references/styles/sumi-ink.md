# sumi-ink

**Look.** A Japanese sumi-e ink painting: black ink on warm washi,
bold brush strokes that swell and thin with pressure, dry-brush
streaks where the brush runs out, soft grey ink washes for shadow and
distance, large areas of untouched paper, and at most one small red
accent where the photo already has red (never an added seal). Economy: the
subject is described with few strokes, the background dissolves into
paper.

**Medium.**
> Repaint this photograph by hand as a Japanese sumi-e ink painting on
> washi paper. It must NOT look like a photograph turned greyscale:
> remove all photographic texture and gradients; everything is black
> brush strokes, grey ink washes and empty paper.

**Prompt block.**
> Japanese sumi-e ink painting of <subject>, black ink brush strokes of
> varying width, dry-brush texture, soft grey ink washes, generous empty
> washi paper, minimal expressive strokes, at most one small red accent
> where the photo already has red, traditional East Asian ink art

**Avoid.** Calligraphy, characters or a seal stamp (say "no
calligraphy, no seal, no text" — the model loves to add some; a seal is
an added object), a greyscale photo with a paper
texture, colour washes beyond the single red accent, manga line art,
a hanging-scroll mock-up or frame, a face so abstracted the identity
is lost.

**QA cues.** The picture is only black, greys, paper and at most one
red; edges are brushed (tapered, dry streaks) not photographic; empty
paper carries part of the composition; the subject's identifying
features survive in a few strokes (glasses stay a continuous frame
stroke); lost colour is the style, not identity drift: judge clothing
and markings by pattern and shape (stripes stay stripes); there are no
written characters and no seal stamp.
