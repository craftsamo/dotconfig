# Comparison

Intent: contrast two (or a small number of) options, states or approaches
so their differences are the visible focus, not a single linear story.

Layout: a stable split composition (side-by-side, or a shared axis with
each option on one side) held for the duration of the units that compare
them; each side gets equal visual weight unless the `learning_goal`
specifically favors one. A unit's `before`/`change`/`after` can map to
"before" being the two starting states and "after" being the resolved
contrast (e.g. a shared outcome metric revealed on both sides at once).

Affordance: a shared reference line, shared scale or synchronized motion
between the two sides makes the difference legible at a glance — a viewer
should be able to compare without re-reading either side from scratch.
Avoid an unexplained camera cut between the two options that breaks the
side-by-side frame.

A custom direction may compare more than two items sequentially, but must
keep each comparison's shared reference visible throughout its hold.

QA: confirm both sides' before/after states remain visible together in the
sampled frames for a shared hold, with neither side dropped from frame
before the contrast resolves.
