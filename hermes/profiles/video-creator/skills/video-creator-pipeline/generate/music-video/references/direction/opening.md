# Opening

Intent: stage the clip like the opening sequence of an animated series -
introduce the world and the cast in quick, music-led glimpses and land on
one key visual that could carry the title. It promises a story rather than
telling one.

Beat guidance: think in three movements across the available duration
(5-15s): the world (a wide establishing view, often with sky or horizon),
the cast (each character gets one short signature glimpse: a turn toward
camera, a run, a look up), and the key visual (everyone or the lead in one
held heroic composition with clear empty space where a title could sit).
Energy usually rises toward the key visual; pace and transition decide the
exact rhythm. With one character, the cast movement is several facets of
that character rather than a line-up. No fixed beat count; a 5s clip may
be only glimpse + key visual.

Prompt example: "Opening-sequence style: the shot opens on a wide view of
<world>. Quick glimpses introduce <character(s)> - <signature action for
each> - as the energy rises, then everything resolves into a held heroic key
visual of <lead or cast> with open sky/space in the <top or side> of the
frame." Rewrite the world, glimpses and key visual around the brief.

Title, credits and logos are never generated here: the key visual leaves
text-free space, and exact lettering is separately agreed finishing (see
the leaf's text policy). Cuts on exact musical beats are not promised.

QA cues: the world is established before the cast; each character's glimpse
is recognizable and on-model; the key visual is held long enough to read and
leaves genuinely empty space for a title; no generated pseudo-lettering in
that space; the sequence reads as an introduction, not a random montage.
