
# Transition: Dissolve

Intent: transition controls boundary grammar - how (or whether) the clip
moves between views - not cut count or motion speed; pace stays an
independent decision. Dissolve means a purposeful overlap between two
shots - short or longer, per the approved proposal's specific duration -
where one view fades out as the next fades in. It is deliberate and
proposal-specified, not an accidental ghost double-exposure or doubled
anatomy left over from an unresolved blend.

Prompt guidance: "The shot dissolves from <view A> into <view B> over a
<short/longer> deliberate overlap, one view fading out as the other fades
in, settling cleanly on the arriving shot with no lingering ghost image."
Rewrite the views and overlap length around the actual approved proposal;
keep the sentence shape, not the exact words.

Avoid: do not apply a dissolve as a silent substitute when the proposal
actually asked for an instantaneous cut - a dissolve changes the requested
grammar and needs its own approval. Do not let the overlap leave a visible
ghost, doubled limb or doubled face hanging in the blended frame; that is a
defect, not an accepted dissolve look. Do not assume a fixed default
overlap duration - the approved proposal sets it, and it never overrides a
relaxed or snappy pace choice for the surrounding action. If successive
on-screen word groups cross the dissolve, clear the prior group before the
next only when the proposal calls for one-at-a-time words; do not impose a
generic no-overlap rule outside that requirement.

QA cues: sampled/timecoded frames through the overlap window are the
available evidence for a clean, ghost-free dissolve - they cannot certify
every unsampled frame of the blend. Flag any visible ghosting, doubled
anatomy or an overlap duration that visibly mismatches what the proposal
specified.
