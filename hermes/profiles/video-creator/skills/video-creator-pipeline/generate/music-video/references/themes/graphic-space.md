# Graphic Space

Space and materials: an abstract, designed void rather than a physical
location - built from geometric volumes and planes (bars, panels, cubes,
discs, grids) arranged in a non-real setting, with deliberate negative space
and no incidental clutter or texture unless placed on purpose. Whether those
volumes/planes render as flat illustrative shapes or as dimensional 3D forms
is a style choice, not fixed here; the space reads as a graphic-design
environment either way, with clean geometry and clear figure/ground
separation.

Default light and palette: lighting defaults to clean and directional - a
single dominant light direction rather than scattered ambient fill. A
flat/even, shadowless illustrative look is one valid option, not mandatory;
when the chosen style calls for dimensional rendering (e.g. anime-3d,
live-action), directional light casting visible shadows and real depth cues
belongs here too and should not be flattened just because the theme is
"graphic." Palette defaults to a bold, limited set (2-4 colors) with strong
contrast between figure and ground, favoring saturated primaries/secondaries
unless the brief specifies otherwise.

Staging opportunities: geometric planes and volumes can slide, swap, rotate
or reveal behind the subject; shapes can occlude or frame part of the
composition as it moves; negative space can isolate the subject before a
plane fills in behind them. These are affordances the space offers, not a
mandated sequence or transition timing - that belongs to the approved
proposal, not this reference.

Adaptation example: a monochrome variant drops the bold multi-color default
down to a single hue plus black and white - value and shape contrast now
carry the composition instead of color contrast, while the geometric
volume/plane vocabulary and reveal/occlusion affordances stay intact
regardless of whether the render leans flat or dimensional. It's still
unmistakably a graphic-space environment, just recomposed around value
instead of hue.

QA cues: geometry (volumes/planes) stays clean and legible, not accidentally
cluttered or textured against its intent; negative space stays deliberate
rather than reading as empty/unfinished; the composition reads as a
coherent designed graphic world across the shot. The presence or absence of
shadows/depth is judged against the chosen style, not scored as a defect -
a dimensional style casting real shadows here is not a violation. Nothing
here should force a render style or character design - those come from the
style reference; transition timing comes from the approved proposal, not
here.
