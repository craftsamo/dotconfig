
# Transition: Whip

Intent: transition controls boundary grammar - how (or whether) the clip
moves between views - not cut count or motion speed; pace stays an
independent decision. Whip means a rapid, directed camera pan that bridges
two views with a purposeful motion blur, then settles cleanly on the
arriving shot. It is a single deliberate directional sweep, not incessant
shake or a jittery handheld wobble, and the pace of the surrounding action
(relaxed through intense) is decided independently of this transition.

Prompt guidance: "The camera whips rapidly from <view A> toward <view B> in
a single directed pan, motion-blurring through the sweep, then settles
cleanly on the arriving framing." Rewrite the views/direction around the
actual brief; keep the sentence shape, not the exact words.

Avoid: do not describe the whip as continuous shake or a jittery handheld
loop; it is one deliberate directional sweep with a clean settle, not
sustained camera noise. Do not let the whip's speed dictate the
surrounding action pace - a whip transition can pair with any pace choice,
and neither one should silently override the other. Do not use whip where
the proposal called for an instantaneous cut or a dissolve; the blur-and-
settle character is specific to this transition.

QA cues: sampled/timecoded frames around the boundary are the available
evidence for a directed blur-then-settle motion, not proof of a perfectly
smooth sweep across every unsampled frame. Flag a whip that never settles
cleanly, or that reads as unstable shake rather than a directed pan.
