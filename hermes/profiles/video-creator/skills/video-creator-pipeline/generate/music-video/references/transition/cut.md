
# Transition: Cut

Intent: transition controls boundary grammar - how (or whether) the clip
moves between views - not cut count or motion speed; pace stays an
independent decision. Cut means an instantaneous shot boundary: one frame
is the old view, the next frame is the new view, with no dissolve, no
ghost overlay and no liquid-morph blending between them. Cut only fires
where the approved proposal actually changes shots (a montage beat, a new
angle); it never forces a performance-direction clip to cut when none was
approved.

Prompt guidance: "The shot cuts instantaneously from <view A> to <view B> -
no dissolve, no crossfade, no morphing blend between the two; each shot
reads as a clean, distinct boundary." Rewrite the two views around the
actual brief; keep the sentence shape, not the exact words.

Avoid: do not let a cut boundary render as a soft dissolve, a ghosted
double-exposure, or a melting morph between shots. A dissolve is an overlap;
a match-cut matches composition or action, not a morph. Do not mandate cutting inside
a single continuous performance beat that the proposal never asked to
break; a cut only appears where the proposal's shot list actually changes.
If successive on-screen word groups are involved, clear the prior word
group before the next appears at the cut boundary - only when the proposal
calls for one-at-a-time words; do not impose a generic no-overlap rule
outside that requirement.

QA cues: sampled/timecoded frames around each boundary are the available
evidence for a clean instantaneous cut - they cannot certify every
unsampled frame, only what was checked. Flag any softness, ghosting or
blend at a boundary meant to be a hard cut.
