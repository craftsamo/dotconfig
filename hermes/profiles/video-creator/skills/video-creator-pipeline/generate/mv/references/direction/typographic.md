# Typographic

Intent: build the shot around large-scale text/lettering as a physical
compositional element - words interacting with depth, moving in front of or
behind other elements, occluding and being occluded, filling or leaving
frame with weight and presence. Typography is treated like a staged object,
not a caption overlay.

Beat guidance: think in terms of the text's spatial journey - where it
enters from, how it relates to depth (in front of the subject, behind it,
sliding between layers), and how it resolves (settles, exits, gets
occluded). A typographic beat is the letterforms doing something physical
across the shot, not appearing instantly fully formed and static. Do not
promise exact spelling, kerning, or precisely timed word-by-word reveals -
current generation models do not reliably render exact text, and this
direction should not be used as if they do.

Prompt example: "Large bold lettering moves through the frame with real
depth - sliding in from <direction>, passing in front of/behind <subject or
set element>, settling into a dominant foreground position as the shot
continues. Treat the text as a solid staged object with weight and
occlusion, not a flat overlay." Adapt the entry direction, depth
relationship and resolution to the brief; do not lock in exact wording here.

QA cues: text/lettering shows real depth interaction (occlusion in both
directions) rather than sitting flat on top of the image like a caption;
motion of the letterforms reads as deliberate staging, not random drift;
exact spelling/timing is NOT graded here - if the brief has a `must_keep`
exact text requirement, that is a planning-stage constraint (defer the
lettering to a text-free base pass and add exact text separately) and this
reference is not the place that guarantees it; flag rather than silently
baking in unverified exact text.
