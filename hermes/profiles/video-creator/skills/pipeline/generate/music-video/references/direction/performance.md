# Performance

Intent: center the shot on an actor's/character's physical performance -
action, facial expression, and a visible evolution of pose or emotion across
the clip. The camera supports the performance; it does not have to move,
cut, or do anything elaborate for this direction to succeed.

Beat guidance: think in terms of a starting emotional/physical state and an
ending one, with the motion in between showing the transition, not two
disconnected poses. A performance beat might be: guarded stance loosening
into openness, a held expression breaking into a reaction, or a gesture
building from small to committed. Camera work is optional support; choose its
cadence from pace (a slow push-in is not the default for snappy). A static
locked frame can serve a performance beat too; only
add camera movement when it reinforces the emotional arc, never as a
substitute for the performance itself. No fixed number of beats is required;
one clear transition across the clip is enough.

Prompt example: "<subject> begins in <starting pose/expression>, and over
the course of the shot <the physical/emotional change> - visible in posture,
face and small gesture, not just a pose swap. Camera holds steady on the
performance (or: camera moves closer with the cadence approved in pace as the
emotion builds)." Rewrite the starting state, the change and the camera
choice around the actual subject and brief; this is a sentence shape, not a
mandatory phrase set.

QA cues: the performance shows a visible arc, not a static pose held for the
whole duration or a single instantaneous change; expression and body
language stay anatomically coherent through the transition; if the camera
moves, the move reads as intentional and in service of the performance, not
incidental drift; no forced multi-shot cutting. Performance defines the focus,
not the edit method: one continuous beat or approved cuts can both serve it.
Follow transition for boundaries and pace for timing, never infer either from
the word performance. A continuous request must not gain cuts.
