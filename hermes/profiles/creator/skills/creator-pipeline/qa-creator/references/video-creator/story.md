# Quality assurance — video-creator: story

Read [common quality assurance](../../SKILL.md) first.

A storyboard is a proposal to review against the premise and viewer: does
it have a setup, a turn and an ending, is every quoted line in the approved
script, is every beat's speaker on screen, and is the audio plan concrete
enough for audio-creator.

For a final, compare the video against the approved storyboard beat by
beat: order and timing (±0.25 s), lines verbatim, each character the same
approved art in every scene (no redrawn or generated stand-in), who is
speaking always clear, captions verbatim and readable at the size of use.
Judge whether the story reads without the premise text and whether the
acting lands (anticipation, reactions, a held beat before the turn). The
helper's PASS covers canvas, duration, audio, true peak, cast bytes and
caption markup; temporal feel, sync and listening stay unverified until a
human watches it. There is no lip sync to judge; a claim of one is a defect.

The look-before-you-answer numbered steps and the verdict/delivery shape are
common — see [common quality assurance](../../SKILL.md).
