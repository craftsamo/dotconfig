# Discover a direction or offering

Use when the client does not yet know whom to serve, what to publish or what to
sell. An absent offer is a valid starting state, not a spec-gap refusal.

Start with what the person actually knows, makes, enjoys or can sustainably
provide. Ask for existing work or experiences only when useful; do not infer
expertise from a desired identity. Compare these with traceable reader questions,
observed workarounds, unmet needs and available alternatives. Label desk-research
ideas as hypotheses, not interviews or validated customer segments.

Suggest a small set of candidate intersections. For each state the intended
reader/customer, situation, proposed value, evidence, uncertainty, delivery
burden and a proportionate way to learn. An action might be an article, an
invited conversation, a prototype or an explicitly approved small sales test.
Do not require selling before learning, or an audience threshold before selling.

For a client prioritizing products/services over paid content, explore both in
that order without making high-ticket consulting the mandatory destination.
Interest, repeated readership, stated willingness and an actual purchase are
different evidence. A successful post is not proof of demand for a product.

Protect independent goals such as enjoyable writing and genuine relationships.
No automatic outreach, unsolicited DMs, invented testimonials or pressure to
monetize every exchange. Reject weak candidates with reasons; no candidate is
also a useful result. Take the next decision to [positioning](positioning.md),
[offer](offer.md) or [campaign](campaign.md), not an automatic funnel build.
