# Positioning

Clarify whose situation matters, what they use instead, the value the client can
actually provide and the evidence supporting that distinction. Include doing
nothing and informal workarounds as alternatives, not only named competitors.

Describe a proposed audience in observable terms: situation, task, constraints,
where the evidence came from and what remains unknown. Do not invent a person's
name, biography, quote or research sample to make a persona look real.

Compare plausible directions on relevance, distinctiveness, credibility and
the client's sustained interest/capacity. These are decision lenses, not a
universal numerical score. Return a recommendation and the strongest reason
against it. A provisional direction is legitimate and can be revised by evidence.

Keep identity consistent without requiring one topic forever or the same voice
on every medium. Do not replace the client's opinions with whatever appears to
perform well. Approved positioning becomes input to [channels](channels.md),
[offer](offer.md) and [campaign](campaign.md), not ready-to-publish copy.
