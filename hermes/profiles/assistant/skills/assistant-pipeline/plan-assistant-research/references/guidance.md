# Guidance — decision surface

Evidence-backed direction for a downstream worker: design
principles, constraints, dos/don'ts, selection rules — derived from
sources and prior results, not the crafted artifact itself. The
researcher owns converting evidence into checkable directives; you supply the
consumer and purpose, and its Plan proposes decision points for Client agreement.

Researcher unit `guidance` · QA `guidance` · units: one consumer's
decision surface per unit; never card-eligible — guidance shapes
work you are about to release, so it stays in the loop.

## Agree before Build

These are proposal choices, not required inputs for a purpose-first Plan.
The caller may author them fully; otherwise Researcher proposes them through
the consuming primary. Follow the parent entry's agreement and preliminary
Build gates; retain same-role unit boundaries.

- **The consumer** — who acts on this (writer, creator, marketer,
  engineer, the user) and what they will produce; an unnamed
  consumer changes what to research and is a spec-gap finding.
- **The decision points** — which choices the guidance must close
  ("hook length, thumbnail composition, and posting cadence" — not
  "how to do YouTube well").
- **The evidence base** — the QA-passed parts, parent results, and
  source classes the directives must derive from; examples to mine
  are named, not discovered.
- **Directive form** — MUST (evidence-strong constraints) / SHOULD
  (recommendations with reasoning) / open choices left to the
  consumer; say when the consumer may deviate.
- **Done criteria** — a stranger could act on the directives
  without reading the sources; every named decision point is
  closed or explicitly left open.
- **Durable path** — guidance feeding a later release lands in a
  file the consuming brief can carry; the reply names it.

## Defaults

- Directives are checkable parameters, not adjectives — "hook
  within 2s", never "start strong".
- Every MUST/SHOULD traces to scored evidence or a named parent
  result; taste without a trace is dropped or labeled opinion.
- Open choices are explicit, with options where useful — silence is
  not a decision.

## Red flags

- The brief wants the artifact too ("guidelines, and draft the
  first script") — split: guidance here, the draft is a writer
  unit consuming it.
- Decision points undecided: specialist Plan proposes them from consumer and
  purpose. Releasing Build while they remain open is a spec-gap finding.
- The evidence base is "whatever you find": heavy breadth is a Searcher
  dependency. Bounded preliminary depth discovery needs primary agreement and
  a separate main proposal; guidance derives from the agreed evidence base.
- Guidance meant to overrule a QA contract or a worker's floors —
  contracts and floors are not researchable opinions.
