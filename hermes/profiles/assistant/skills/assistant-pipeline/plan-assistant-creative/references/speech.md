# Speech - words for a listener

## Use

Spoken words delivered as an asset, including editing or findings on an
existing recording. An ordinary conversational spoken reply is not this
durable-media workflow. Writing the words remains Writer's responsibility
when it is a released writing task; singing is not assumed to be speech.

## Client decisions

Identify listener, purpose, approved words and pronunciation concerns. Keep
exact text separate from production notes. Preserve an explicitly chosen
voice/character identity and stated delivery intent; do not substitute another
voice to work around a failure. If the script is unsettled, note the
dependency for Execute rather than synthesizing provisional words as a finished input.
Relay known permission for any external processing separately from the voice
choice and ask the hands what remains necessary.

## References

Do not make visual design research mandatory for speech. Relay the user's
own voice examples or descriptions with provenance and allowed use; no
unverified claim that you listened or that a person consented to imitation.

## Acceptance

Compare approved words with the hands' readback and measurement evidence.
Keep pronunciation and performance uncertainty distinct from an ASR spelling
variant. Request the user's listening judgment when needed; neither a file
nor a transcript alone proves the requested delivery or identity.
Ask about the specific unmet performance criterion on the exact delivered
candidate. Record the file/version, criterion and user's actual observation
as user-reported evidence, not your own listening verdict. General acceptance
alone does not prove that criterion or replace technical/source checks.
