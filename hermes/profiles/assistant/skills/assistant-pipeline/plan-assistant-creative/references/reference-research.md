# Reference research - evidence for a direction

Help the user and Creator choose a direction using real examples. You collect,
inspect and translate observations into concrete visual intent; Creator turns
that design into a feasible technical production proposal. A
reference is evidence of an example, not proof that this production can match
it and not a ready-made production specification.

## When

Research when the user asks for examples, direction is open and comparison
would help, or competing directions need grounding. Skip extra searching for
supplied concrete references, a settled small revision or a fully specified
simple asset. A mood or genre word is a search clue, not a stored template.
Use the selected Client guide's References section as a lens, not a checklist.

A research-only request ends with the comparison. Do not generate samples,
start production or spend a media allowance. Existing search/browser/vision
permissions still apply; research is not a promise of offline inference.

## Find and inspect

1. Identify what the reference should help decide: information hierarchy,
   visual identity, mood, motion or a particular use context. Start with
   2-4 candidates overall, not a separate quota for every open dimension.
2. Search relevant public examples: product pages, portfolios, galleries,
   published images, motion reels or authorized supplied references. Prefer
   the user's actual audience/use over an unrelated fashionable surface.
   Wide enumeration or X-heavy collection uses the existing
   [search plan](../../plan-assistant-search/SKILL.md) and [search execution](../../execute-assistant-search/SKILL.md),
   not a new creative card. Do not scrape logged-in surfaces or inspect
   private records merely to collect visual inspiration.
3. Inspect the actual page/image or available video evidence. Record the URL,
   access date, what was inspected and any local capture path. A search
   snippet is only a lead. If the source cannot be inspected, mark it
   uninspected and do not invent colors, layout or motion from its description.
4. Extract observations, relevance and cautions. Screenshots support visible
   composition, not motion timing; sampled frames do not prove a whole film.
   Audio examples need appropriate returned analysis evidence or the user's
   description; never invent having listened. Reuse existing captures rather
   than automatically repeating a search or remote analysis.
5. Compare the useful differences and suggest a direction, or ask the user
   the choice that matters. Preserve which choices are undecided. If search
   is thin, say so and ask Creator for a proposal from the known brief; do
   not block all planning or manufacture examples.

## Reference Pack

For visual-story design, use three complementary kinds of evidence, not an
unbounded gallery or a mandatory new search on every job:

- Finished motion with process: [Ordinary Folk](https://www.ordinaryfolk.co/process)
  and [BUCK](https://buck.co/work/google-pixel-made-you-look) are starting points
  for composition, intermediate states and coordinated motion. Use relevant
  inspected work, never assume a studio name proves the requested quality.
- Actual UI and its rationale: [Linear's redesign](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)
  and [Raycast's UI update](https://www.raycast.com/blog/launch-week-summary)
  expose component anatomy, hierarchy, density, lettering and states. They are
  dated examples, not current-product claims or mandatory dark/minimal styling.
  A beautiful landing page is not evidence for its unseen application controls.
- Film construction: [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/severance/)
  can explain visual metaphors, shot connections and experiments. Transfer the
  relevant relationship, not film-length pacing into an unrelated short tour.

Use existing media-craft knowledge to read these examples, not instead of
examining them. Creator separately uses technical demonstrations such as GSAP,
Three.js, Codrops or Shadertoy to evaluate implementation. Neither a demo nor
its documentation establishes support in the selected hands' actual runtime.
Accounts, subscriptions, downloads and code/asset reuse need their own rights;
do not make a paid or login-only library a hidden prerequisite.

For each important adopted observation, identify its scene/event/component in
the proposed design, the observable mechanism and why it fits the audience.
"Like Linear" or "smooth like a studio reel" is not a design decision. Inspect
individual parts and transitions, not just the poster or the best hero frame.
Separate inferred timing from observed timing; never claim a sampled sequence
was watched continuously. Reuse previously inspected examples with their limits;
add a targeted lookup when a real design question remains. These starting points
are learning material, not a stored recipe or an exhaustive quality whitelist.

Carry a compact, self-contained pack in the brief; use files only when the
evidence warrants them. No new registry or mandatory sidecar schema:

```text
References:
  R1: source URL, access date, inspected page/image/frames, capture path if any
      observed: visible traits actually checked; limits of that check
      relevance: what this could contribute to this job
      application: scene/event/component, mechanism and reason; original proposals labeled
      avoid: what should not be inherited, and why
      role: inspiration only; production reuse/upload not authorized
Direction:
  suggested: a synthesis to discuss, with its reference evidence
  user-decided: only choices the user actually settled
  open: what the user or Creator should still decide
```

An approved direction is not approval of every pixel, source asset or later
proposal. Keep the granted creative discretion and mandatory proposal gates
from [execution](../../execute-assistant-creative/SKILL.md), not a new approval ceremony
for every research suggestion.

## Rights and permissions

References inspire; they are not automatically production assets. Browsing,
copyright/license permission to reuse, model-upload consent, remote-analysis
consent and permission to publish are separate questions. Public availability,
a local copy, a chosen style or "use this" does not settle them all. Do not
upload a found example to a production model or copy its protected artwork,
footage or exact text merely because it appears in the pack.

If an example is separately authorized as an input, explicitly relay the
asset's role and allowed operations; preserve all unknown or denied rights.
Creator requests missing permission before the affected operation, not after
an upload. Neither Assistant's brief shape nor a capture path conveys consent.
Keep private asset identities in working detail, never public deliverables.

Job evidence remains with that job. Do not promote an accepted look into a
standing format, device catalog or compulsory audiovisual recipe.
