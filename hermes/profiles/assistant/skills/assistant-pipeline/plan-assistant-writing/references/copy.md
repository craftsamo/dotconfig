# Marketing copy — decision surface

Reader-facing persuasive text produced as a **part** for marketing
or web-content work: LP body copy, promotional announcements,
mail-sequence text. X/Instagram posts and captions use
`post.md` and the Writer post leaves, not this copy route. This leaf
covers other text the writer drafts for later consumption.

Posts for other social platforms keep marketing Execute's existing drafting
contract; do not route them to Copy merely because they are long.

Factual release notes use the document family in `documentation.md`; do not
route them here merely because they announce a change.

Direct correspondence and service/transaction notifications use `message.md`.
Promotional mail remains copy: select by purpose, not the email medium or length.

Writer type `copy` (including existing `marketing-copy` briefs) · QA `prose` · units: whole small job;
piece units for a set (LP sections, a mail sequence).

Select `write-copy` for new copy, `edit-copy` for an existing draft with named
changes, and `analyze-copy` for observations about an existing target. Read the
selected form; destinations are landing-page, email, announcement or a described
custom destination. An absent target calls for planning consultation, not
imaginary analysis. A bounded analysis need not supply a full writing brief.

## Fix before release

- **The claim** — the one message this copy carries, taken from the
  campaign's message unit or the approved plan; copy never invents
  its own angle.
- **Claim sources** — in a marketing context every factual claim,
  number, testimonial, and proof point resolves to the project's
  fact ledger (`../../plan-assistant-marketing/references/marketing-state.md`); paste the
  entries into the brief. A proof slot with no ledger entry is left
  empty by design, not filled by eloquence — unsupported required claims block
  the unit; text defects return to Writer rather than being removed.
- **Audience + destination constraints** — reader, platform, hard
  length limits, required disclosures (mail: unsubscribe context).
- **Action** — required only when the released purpose calls for action; supply
  its actual target. An awareness announcement need not have a CTA.
- **Voice** — tone anchored to the positioning record's voice;
  required and banned terms.
- **Done criteria** — e.g. "LP copy explains the approved benefit and offer
  conditions, with claims supported by the fact ledger".
- **Offer and protection** — actual prices, eligibility, dates and disclosures;
  identify protected wording. Edits need an original and authorized scope;
  offer corrections require supporting evidence, not just a tone preference.
- **Reference roles** — sources ground claims; a style_reference and
  reference_focus guide expression, not facts or endorsements. Humanizer is
  explicit-request only and never overrides protected terms.

## Defaults

- Tone from the project's positioning record when one exists.
- Length from the destination medium.
- Promotional claims: facts from the brief only — scope is never embellished.
- A mail sequence is decomposed as piece units per message, with
  the sequence's course boundary and offer cadence decided in the
  campaign plan (`../../plan-assistant-marketing/references/campaign.md`), not by the writer.

## Red flags

- "Just write something punchy" — the claim is undecided; spec-gap.
- Copy expected to produce its own proof — fabrication by
  assignment.
- A required action whose target is undecided — return that gap; do not invent
  a destination or force a CTA into a purpose that does not call for one.
- A mandatory claim conflicting with evidence — clarify before drafting;
  do not manufacture proof or silently weaken an approved promise.
- X/Instagram post copy sent through this route instead of `post.md`.
- Direct correspondence or service notifications sent through copy merely
  because they use email — those use the Message family in `message.md`.
