# Campaign — decomposition archetype

Turns an approved strategy into released units. A campaign plan is
an ordered set of **message units** (posts/threads with settled
claims, audiences, destinations) plus **part dependencies** (long
copy → writer, media → creator, evidence → research), released one
unit at a time under the frontier rule. Preparation decides the
outcome — assume results are mostly fixed before anything ships,
and plan accordingly.

## Decompose

1. **Concepts before calendar** — generate several campaign
   concepts, ground them (marketer judgment, precedent research —
   no precedent anywhere is a warning, not an opportunity), select
   with the user; keep the losers as a portfolio (bad timing is not
   a dead idea).
2. **Message units** — per unit: the one claim, its fact-ledger
   references, audience segment, destination, part needs, and the
   measurement it feeds. One claim per unit; a unit that needs two
   claims is two units.
3. **Calendar** — cadence honest to the maturity stage; unit order
   respects part dependencies and the education-before-ask rule.
4. **Part specs** — fixed before requesting production, per
   deliverable type: LP → transformation promise, offer terms,
   single conversion action, objection coverage, legal paths; mail
   sequence → themes, course boundary, offer cadence, consent and
   segmentation; article → one claim, primary-experience evidence,
   search intent, next-step destination; video → reusable format,
   single topic, CTA destination; community → interaction
   structures, rituals, safety rules. (Long-form production is the
   writer's; these are the decisions its brief carries.)

## Launch shape — for a product/offer release

| Phase | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-launch | publish product detail early and continuously; build an explicit intent list; disclose scope, timing, and usually price so prospects self-qualify |
| Just before | spaced reminders to the intent list (a week / a day / at release), a preview event concentrating attention — never reminder spam |
| Launch day | operate the release as a live event across owned + discovery channels; amplify authentic reviews and real customer discussion |
| Just after | add planned value, follow-up education, keep capturing subscribers; a genuine price rise is announced selectively and transparently |
| Later | transition to evergreen acquisition feeding the sequence and backend — launch is a beginning, not an endpoint |

A timed onboarding sequence (free educational course → immediate
benefit → final offer aligned to the subscriber's registration
context, purchasers segmented from non-purchasers) is the standing
machine the launch fills.

## Inspection standard — before presenting for approval

- Every unit's claim resolves to the fact ledger; every part has a
  producing capability and a QA contract.
- The decomposition matches the maturity stage and checks clean
  against the non-goals list.
- Measurement is named per unit (what it feeds in the KPI tree).

## Red flags

- Multi-channel decomposition for a pre-traction project.
- Fabricated scarcity, false countdowns, perpetual "discounts".
- Reach maximization that would recruit unqualified, soon-unhappy
  buyers; launch cadence that fatigues the audience or the user.
- Automation built around an offer without durable market fit.
- A "campaign" that is really an undecided strategy — send it back
  to the positioning/offer leaves, not into production.
