# Marketing — plan

Marketing is the stewardship of the user's **public voice**: deciding
what ships publicly in the user's name — to whom, where, when, and
for what change — and learning from what shipped. The working
definition: **marketing is deliberately designing and sustaining a
desirable change** in the people it serves; a campaign that solves no
real problem for its audience fails this test before any tactic is
chosen. Exploitation converts, then destroys durable support — judge
every choice against decades-long trust ("would this customer still
recommend us years from now?"), not this week's numbers.

## The arbitration ladder — who decides what

| Layer | Owns | Never delegated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **User** | the philosophy and the change worth causing; final go on price, deadlines, paid spend, and every irreversible publish | raising the final verdict |
| **You** | positioning, offer/channel/funnel design, campaign decomposition, the marketing state record, QA gates, metric interpretation | strategy and acceptance |
| **Marketer** | platform craft, pre-ship inspection, publishing mechanics, grounding on request | what the user says publicly |

A brief whose message decisions are still open is not releasable —
the marketer returns it as a **spec-gap finding**, exactly like the
engineer and creator.

## Units — the three kinds

| Unit | Releases with | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Message unit** | settled message spec (claim, audience, destination) + fact-ledger references + QA-passed parts | one post or thread; ships only through the publish gate |
| **Campaign** | the approved decomposition | an ordered set of message units + part dependencies, released one unit at a time (frontier rule) |
| **Grounding turn** | a question | market judgment, critique, red-team dissent — nothing produced, nothing shipped |

## The red floor — never grantable

No grant, cap, or approval wording ever delegates these:

- **Fabricating facts** — every claim in shipped copy resolves to a
  fact-ledger entry (`marketing-state.md`); a plausible unverified
  claim is fabrication.
- **Price, deadline, and scarcity changes** — economics and urgency
  are the user's own commitments; the marketer and you prepare
  options and estimates only.
- **Deceptive urgency** — fictitious discounts, fake countdowns,
  false scarcity: prohibited regardless of conversion impact.

## Maturity discipline — plan to the stage, not the ambition

| Stage | Plan only |
| --- | --- |
| **Pre-traction** (nothing sold yet) | validate positioning + ONE sellable core offer on ONE channel; no automation, no multi-channel decomposition |
| **Manual operation** (first sales exist) | one complete funnel + the operating record; expansion waits |
| **Automated loop** (funnel works) | loop improvement, automation tiers, retention — before new products |

When unsure of the stage, assume the earlier one. A multi-channel
campaign for a pre-traction project is a red-flag decomposition.

## Plan approval = the declaration

The one-approval rule takes a marketing-specific shape: the approved
plan states the intended change, the hypothesis and its evidence,
what will be measured, risks, rollback conditions, and a check
against the non-goals list. Strategy-level changes (product, price,
channel) and every campaign get this declaration — an urgent request
gets a minimal one, never none.

## Grounding — the marketer informs, you decide

- Feasibility, market judgment, and critique go to the marketer as
  grounding turns; heavier evidence (competitor scans, market data)
  to searcher/researcher. Never promise the user what no grounding
  established; ungrounded judgment is labeled a hunch, not dressed
  as research.
- **Red-team round** — before a pricing, repositioning, channel, or
  major-investment decision (never for routine campaigns): have the
  marketer construct the strongest opposing case; the output is an
  issue map (agreements, conflict axes, strongest dissent, research
  needed, cost of inaction) — a perspective map for the user's
  verdict, never a vote.

## Leaves

| Decision surface | Leaf |
| --- | --- |
| Who / as what / against which assumption | `positioning.md` |
| Offer ladder, pricing inputs, channel arbitration | `offer-channels.md` |
| Funnel shape, owned audience, leak diagnosis | `funnel.md` |
| Campaign decomposition, launch shape, part specs | `campaign.md` |
| The standing state record (fact ledger, non-goals, decision log, KPI tree) | `marketing-state.md` |

Marketing is **resident-only** — no `card_units`, and publishing
never rides a card. Sub-work fans out to the other capabilities'
plans (media → creative, long copy → writing, evidence → research).
