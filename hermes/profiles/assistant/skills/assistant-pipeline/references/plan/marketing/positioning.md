# Positioning — decision surface

Resolved BEFORE any offer, price, or channel decision. Positioning is
the standing answer to *who, as what, against which assumption* — it
outlives campaigns and lives in the state record
(`marketing-state.md`). Skipping it and planning a campaign anyway is
the classic failure: tactics optimizing an undefined change.

## Fix with the user

- **Audience by desired change, not demographics** — the smallest
  group genuinely motivated by the transformation; write it as one
  before → after sentence, not a feature list.
- **Identity** — one role, voice, and promise held constant across
  every channel; products may vary, identities may not. The role
  must match both genuine conviction and demonstrated demand — and
  carry credibility evidence (lived experience, outcomes, artifacts).
  If the user would not personally want the transformation, stop.
- **The adversary** — a harmful assumption, system, or convention;
  never a competitor or a person.
- **Territory** — narrow the audience, problem, and domain until a
  credible small-market leadership position exists; prefer a stable
  problem space over dependence on one volatile tool or platform.
  Expand through adjacent ground only after the niche is satisfied.
- **Brand posture** — brand is accumulated trust and expectation,
  not a logo: what belief does it stand for, and how do customers
  who share it participate? Manage expectations actively; correct
  inflated ones before delivery fails them.

## Community and culture (when a community exists or is planned)

- A community needs member-to-member relationships — an audience
  that only watches the user is fans, not members.
- Culture = shared values and repeated behaviors; verbalize the
  desired ones early in concise, memorable language, then reinforce
  through leader behavior and operating decisions — it is directed,
  not controlled, and it is the hardest differentiation to copy.
- Community sits AFTER trust and purchase in the funnel — a
  retention and participation layer, not an acquisition channel.

## Validation and revisit

- Positioning is validated by customers' own language and reported
  transformations — wording only the user likes is an unvalidated
  hypothesis. Ground with marketer judgment turns and real audience
  research (never fabricated personas).
- Revisit when: content categories fragment, buyers differ from the
  engaged audience, publishing feels chronically misaligned, or the
  underlying need has been fulfilled.

## Red flags

- Generalist positioning without breadth itself being the valued
  specialty.
- A transformation sentence that is really a feature list.
- Multiple identities across channels "to test what works".
- A community planned as a growth channel instead of a
  post-purchase layer.
