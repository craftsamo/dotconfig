# Funnel & owned audience — decision surface

The flow model every campaign hangs on: **awareness → lead capture →
education → sale → retention/community**, powered by three loops —
acquisition, conversion, retention — with referral feeding retention
back into acquisition. Design backward from the durable flagship
offer at the bottom; content and follower growth are inputs, never
the strategy.

## Design rules

- **Never sell a high-consideration offer through broad reach** —
  education builds the trust the sale needs; an inexpensive,
  genuinely satisfying first purchase creates commitment before the
  flagship ask.
- **Backend-first**: pick the durable evergreen product first, then
  design acquisition so it never needs rebuilding per campaign.
- Broad channels create awareness; the owned list carries education
  and conversion (two-layer rule, `offer-channels.md`).

## Owned audience — the backbone asset

- The mail list is the business's durable, platform-independent
  asset; build it **concurrently** with content, never "after the
  audience grows". Every content/campaign plan names its
  list-capture path (lead incentive, sequenced education).
- Qualified opt-ins over vanity reach: measure lead quality by
  source and prioritize sources with stronger downstream behavior.
  Organic before paid until conversion economics are evidenced.
- **Easy unsubscribe is strategy**: it filters for genuinely engaged
  long-term customers and protects sender trust (and 特電法
  compliance).
- No backend offer yet → run a media-style recurring newsletter and
  keep acquiring; automation adds nothing without qualified inflow.

## Leak diagnosis — one leak at a time

When growth stalls, locate the weakest stage before choosing any
tactic:

1. List the stage metrics; an **unmeasured stage is the first
   leak** — instrument before diagnosing.
2. Find the largest stage-to-stage drop; fix that one leak, then
   remeasure. Never ship a bundle of unrelated initiatives.
3. Weak sales conversion → check educational exposure and trust
   BEFORE touching copy or price. Weak interest → deepen repeated
   long-form contact, not more reach. Quality blocks conversion →
   fix the product, not the promotion.
4. Purchase pages: audit for uncertainty, missing information, and
   friction — mundane leaks outrank clever tactics.
5. Judge each channel separately on arrival volume vs
   landing-conversion; apply the remedy matching the weak dimension.

Post-purchase is a stage, not an exit: advocacy, reviews, and
community participation are designed, and purchase data is retained
(where consent allows) for retention, exclusion, and relevant
offers.

## Red flags

- A funnel drawn top-down from "grow the account" instead of
  backward from the flagship.
- List building deferred until "the content works".
- A diagnosis that names no measured stage — tactics chosen by mood.
- Paid amplification before the organic loop demonstrably converts.
