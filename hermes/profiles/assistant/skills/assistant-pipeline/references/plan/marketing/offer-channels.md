# Offer ladder & channel arbitration — decision surface

Two coupled decisions downstream of positioning: what is sold (and
for how much), and where. **Price is the user's own commitment** —
you and the marketer prepare tiers, estimates, and evidence; the
number itself is raised by the user (red floor).

## Offer ladder — design backward from the core

- Start from the **high-value core offer** that completes the
  customer transformation for a limited cohort; derive everything
  else from the trust and education that offer requires.
- Ladder shape: free entry point → low-price self-contained product
  → mid-price product bundled with a time-bounded community → core.
  Every rung must be independently satisfying while building
  readiness for the next.
- **At inception, launch only the free entry point and the core** —
  intermediate rungs are added after real sales validate demand.
- High-ticket programs are discouraged: when value is hard to
  substantiate or incentives turn extractive, they trade the brand
  for revenue. Consider only where informed buyers would judge the
  price proportionate.
- Payment model follows the value shape: one-time when the value is
  complete; recurring only when delivery is genuinely continuous.

## Pricing inputs — prepared, never decided

Present the user a documented basis: gross margin, LTV, acquisition
cost, fixed-cost break-even, realistic reach, and conversion
assumptions — plus the anchor questions: "what would justify ten
times this price?" and "is this discount strategy or insecurity?".
Underpricing against comparable offers without a strategic reason is
a finding, not a default. Claimed scope, proof, testimonials,
deadlines, capacity, and guarantees in any offer copy must resolve
to fact-ledger entries.

## Channel arbitration — four axes

Score every sales/distribution channel on:

1. **Distribution cost** — fees are acceptable when they buy access
   to otherwise unreachable buyers; minimizing fees is not a goal.
2. **Policy risk** — platform rules and their volatility; record
   dated policy-risk notes in the state record and revalidate.
3. **List ownership** — does the channel yield contacts the user can
   reach if the platform disappears? Owned and exportable beats
   rented reach.
4. **Audience proximity** — is the intended audience actually there,
   and can discovery plausibly influence a purchase?

Structure: **two layers** — discovery channels for reach, owned
contact channels (mail list) for education and core-offer sales.
Channel roles: social feeds = top-funnel awareness routed into the
owned list; long-form/audio/video = depth and affinity; search and
AI-search surfaces = primary information plus identifiable expert
opinion, never summaries; PR = creating the conditions for credible
third parties to speak. Messaging-app channels win where they are
culturally standard or the audience skews local/younger; email wins
on volume, analytics, portability, and automation — running both
requires distinct purposes.

Adding, migrating, or exiting a channel is a strategy-level
declaration: limited real-sales test first, existing customers'
treatment stated, never a wholesale simultaneous move. Affiliates
and partners get written claim guidelines — the user remains
accountable for their promotion. Token/stakeholder mechanics are a
future channel class: legally sensitive, specialist review before
any planning.

## Red flags

- An offer ladder invented before the core offer exists.
- Pricing "decided" inside a session — options yes, commitments no.
- A new channel adopted because it is trendy, with no arbitration
  scoring or sales test.
- Discount-and-urgency mechanics standing in for a value argument.
