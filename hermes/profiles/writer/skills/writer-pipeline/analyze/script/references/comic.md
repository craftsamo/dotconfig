# Analyzing comic scripts

Use panel/page IDs and speaker/field quotations to discuss story structure,
dialogue, attribution or continuity. Evaluate producer requirements only when
the relevant contract is supplied; a bounded dialogue question needs no artwork.

A textual action description is not a rendered panel. Without images, do not
certify balloon fit, pose clarity, visual continuity or exact lettering in art.
Do not infer a panel's missing context from a desired story resolution.

QA: comparisons inspect both scripts; observations and proposed improvements
are distinct. The analysis report does not need its own panels, lettering or
replacement dialogue and never changes the target's IDs or approved story.

## Craft Decisions

Inspect whether each panel names a visible beat, its actor and the speaker of
each line. Distinguish essential visual information from dialogue's response
or subtext; repetition is a finding only when it impairs the requested purpose.
If several actions compete for a supplied panel, quote them and explain the
staging uncertainty, without drawing new panels or changing structure/IDs.
Give findings without replacement dialogue. Descriptive-plan coverage, when
required, is separate from whether the final lettering or pose works visually.

## Worked Example (Fictional, MATERIAL-COMPLETE)

Supplied material: fictional P10 then P20; Jo hands Mina a key, then Mina
unlocks the door. Jo is courteous and Mina terse. Review only action clarity
and speaker attribution. No artwork or lettering limits are supplied.
Creative-fiction allowance: this handoff story, not real events or new motives.
Supplied draft:
```text
P10 | Action: Hope changes hands. | Speaker: Jo | Dialogue: Here you are.
P20 | Action: Mina unlocks the door. | Speaker: Mina | Dialogue: Thanks.
```
Finding: P10's "Hope changes hands" does not identify a drawable object/actor.
Evidence: the supplied material names Jo, Mina and the key, but P10's action
does not. Consequence: the artist must invent the handoff that enables P20.
Rationale: this is an action-field omission, not proof that Jo's line is weak.
Retain: "Here you are" and "Thanks" fit the supplied voices and attribution;
do not demand that both characters recite all visible information.
Counterexample: a poetic caption can use this phrase if action specifies the handoff.
QA: cite P10's field and compare it with P20 and the material; keep original
IDs, panel structure and words unchanged. No new panels or corrected lines.
Textual beat inspectability is unmet here; actual balloon fit, pose clarity
and production success remain unverified without the corresponding evidence.
