# Analyzing screenplays

Describe scene structure, dialogue, character knowledge or continuity with
scene/field quotations. Distinguish scripted facts within a fictional world
from interpretations of motivation and from claims about real people.

For comparison, read both versions and use the requested criteria; do not
invent an ending or pick a winner when the question asks only for description.
Actor response, audience emotion and production feasibility are not established
by a compelling line in the text.

QA: the report's observations are grounded and its limits explicit. It needs
no new cast, dialogue or scene units, and it does not rewrite the screenplay
or claim a performed duration, casting decision or approved production.

## Craft Decisions

Compare action/dialogue with supplied intent; separate inferred subtext from
confirmed motives. Inspect distinct SUPPLIED voice without inventing biography.
If a character explains facts both speakers already know, quote the exposition
and explain its effect on the exchange, not an imagined audience reaction.
Preserve screenplay form; give findings without replacement lines or identities.
Unfilmable commentary can be a planning note, not sufficient staged action.

## Worked Example (Fictional, MATERIAL-COMPLETE)

Supplied material: SC10, INT. HALL - DAY; Ada wants Ben to stay without asking.
Ada holds the door open; Ben pauses at it. Both know he is leaving. Ada uses
short questions, Ben full polite sentences. Review dialogue against that intent.
Creative-fiction allowance: this scene's dialogue, not real quotes or new motives.
Supplied draft:
```text
SC10 - INT. HALL - DAY
Ada holds the door open. Ben pauses at it.
ADA
As you know, you are leaving. Are you leaving already?
BEN
Yes, I should go.
```
Finding: Ada's "As you know, you are leaving" states shared knowledge.
Evidence: the material says both know this; her following question already
addresses departure. Consequence: exposition delays the exchange's pressure.
Rationale: the finding concerns dialogue function, not Ada's psychology.
Retain: the door and pause support subtext; Ben's polite sentence fits his voice.
Counterexample: repetition can be an accusation if that intent is supplied, not guessed.
QA: quote SC10 and the intent/voice material; leave all source fields unchanged.
Acting, emotion and duration remain unverified; no replacement dialogue is delivered.
