# Analyzing landing-page copy

Read the actual headings, claims, evidence, terms and actions. Explain their
relationship using quotations; do not mistake a familiar persuasion pattern
for evidence that this page converts. A description can explain the structure
without recommending a redesign.

For review, compare stated benefits and qualifications with the supplied brief
and evidence. A screenshot or rendered page may provide additional context;
plain text alone cannot establish visual hierarchy, responsive fit or controls.
Do not run a form submission or analytics experiment to fill that gap.

## Craft decisions

Trace audience need, approved benefit, substantiation, adjacent terms and any
purpose-dependent CTA through the supplied copy. A fixed persuasion formula
does not establish fit; an informational page can legitimately have no action.
For review, quote the claim, compare evidence, identify a mismatch and explain
its possible consequence. A condition elsewhere cannot repair a contradictory
headline. If evidence is missing rather than contradictory, preserve that distinction.
Describe the relationship without replacement copy, invented scarcity or results.

## Worked example

Fictional material: headline 「すべてのプランで1週間の予約をまとめて確認」 and
adjacent terms 「Team プラン限定」. The supplied feature sheet confirms weekly
display only on Team; the audience is comparing plans. Review claim consistency.
Finding: quote 「すべてのプラン」 against both the adjacent Team-only term and
the feature sheet. Mismatch: universal eligibility contradicts the known restriction.
Consequence: a reader comparing plans could expect the feature on an ineligible
plan. The adjacent disclosure does not make the headline's assertion accurate.
Why: the report links exact claim and substantiation to a decision-relevant
problem without proposing a new headline or asserting conversion loss.

Retain: a capability claim with matching terms needs no invented testimonial
or CTA when the purpose is informational. Counterexample: treating lack of
scarcity language as a defect substitutes a formula for the actual brief.

QA evidence: preserve the quote, supporting passage and contradictory term;
explain the bounded consequence for the stated audience. Mark visual adjacency
unverified if only text order was supplied, and leave actual performance unknown.
