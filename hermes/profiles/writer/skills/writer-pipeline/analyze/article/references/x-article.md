# Analyzing X Articles

Assess a complete article rather than imposing single-post brevity or thread
structure. Use the requested purpose to examine the opening, progression and
supported claims; engagement and conversion do not follow from wording alone.

X Articles use a dedicated rich-text editor. A source draft does not prove
Markdown import, working media embeds or current account eligibility.
Documented editor features include headings, lists, bold, italic and links,
plus media and X-post embeds. These do not establish Markdown paste/import
conversion. Code/table/quote-block support and the alt/caption workflow remain
unverified in this check; an indentation feature is not proof of a quote block.
Keep text analysis separate from unperformed editor/visual checks. Do not
publish a test or fetch account analytics under an article-analysis request.

Documentary source (checked 2026-09-08):
https://help.x.com/en/using-x/articles

## Inspect Promise and Standalone Context

If asked about clarity or article structure, compare the title promise with
the evidence actually delivered. Read a section opening alone to test compact
standalone context, then in place to avoid flagging a clear local reference.
Name the subject or condition a reader would need to recover; do not require
a promotional hook or assume the article needs conversion-oriented copy.

Locally authored example (hypothetical title and body):
Title quote: "Make every page load instantly."
Body quote: "Reusing a saved copy can avoid fetching the page again."
Finding: the title promises universal instant loading, while the body describes
one conditional mechanism with no timing evidence. Reader impact: the expected
outcome exceeds the explanation's support. Recommend bounding the promise or
supplying relevant evidence, not a replacement title or article. An opening
"This solves it" adds a context problem only if its referents are unavailable.

Retain: a plain opening that identifies its subject, useful section headings
and necessary technical detail. A missing follow appeal is not a defect.
An article may be compact without being post-sized; do not recommend a thread
solely because the source is long. Compare passages by shared reader criteria,
not by an assumed engagement formula or a predicted audience response.

QA evidence: quote title and supporting or conflicting body text, identify
the scope difference and reader impact, and mark missing timing evidence as
unverified. Distinguish a section-context observation from an excerpt you did
not inspect. Keep source and notes unchanged and deliver no replacement article;
source analysis establishes neither editor rendering nor account eligibility.
