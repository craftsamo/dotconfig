# Analyzing a proposal

Identify the requested decision, the alternatives actually presented and
the stated comparison criteria. Examine whether evidence and uncertainty
support the proposal's conclusions without supplying a position of your own
unless recommendations were requested.

For comparisons, distinguish different goals or constraints before ranking
options. An exploratory memo can legitimately leave a decision open. Do not
call missing approval a writing defect or treat estimated effects as promises.

## Examine how the comparison supports the decision

Locate the decision sought and any supplied provisional stance. Compare the
alternatives on the same axes, including units, assumptions, reasons and
conditions that make a trade-off matter. Assess whether the text supports its
own stance, not which option you prefer. These guides do not require a winner,
budget or implementation plan for an exploratory discussion.

Build a finding from quote to source/evidence, mismatch and consequence for
the requested decision. Distinguish an actual defect from missing evidence
and optional preference about presentation. A correction direction may name
the unsupported axis or missing condition, but must not become a replacement
draft, invented cost estimate or recommendation the requester did not seek.

## Worked example and retain condition

Fictional teaching material: a memo says "Recordings are the cheaper choice."
The supplied option notes compare live and recorded training on access and
feedback, list costs as unknown, and give only a provisional preference for
recordings if delayed answers are acceptable.

Quote: "Recordings are the cheaper choice."
Source: the option notes provide no cost comparison and condition the stance
on feedback needs.
Mismatch: the conclusion invents a cost ranking in place of the stated reason.
Consequence: readers may approve the pilot on an unsupported financial basis.
Recommend obtaining common-axis cost evidence or limiting the conclusion to
the supplied reasoning; do not choose the format or rewrite the proposal.

Retain a conditional preference when its condition and reasons are clear.
Unknown costs limit cost ranking, not discussion of access. A table instead
of comparable prose is optional preference; an open choice is not a defect.
Without the option notes, report missing evidence rather than a proven cost error.

QA: findings assess the released decision and its evidence; keep an unsupported
claim distinct from lack of approval or a preference for another option.

QA: observations refer to the text; reader effects and recommendations are
labeled interpretations. The report neither authorizes the proposal nor
silently turns it into a revised implementation plan.
