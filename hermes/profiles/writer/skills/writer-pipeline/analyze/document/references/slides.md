# Analyzing a slide outline

Read the written sequence, slide roles, claims, evidence and separation of
visible text from speaker notes. Identify slides by their existing IDs.
Do not demand a fixed number of bullets, a new conclusion on every slide or
a closing CTA when the agreed purpose does not require one.

An outline alone cannot establish visual legibility, layout fit, pacing or
spoken duration. Supplied screenshots or measurements support only the
properties actually observed; missing graphics remain unavailable evidence.

## Follow the visible qualified argument

Distinguish topic, message line, evidence and speaker notes using the actual
producer fields. Read argument-bearing message lines in sequence to see
whether the qualified argument survives without hidden narration. A topic
title may coexist with a clear message; agenda and transition roles need no
new claim. These guides impose no fixed slide count or bullet template.

Build a finding from quote to source/evidence, mismatch and consequence for
the audience's understanding. Separate an actual defect in claim scope from
missing evidence about rendering and optional preference for layout. Identify
the needed correction, not a replacement draft or deck. Keep stable slide IDs
in findings so a producer can locate the issue without an invented reorder.

## Worked example and retain condition

Fictional teaching material: S-entry's message says "Entry is faster for
everyone." Its evidence is a supplied pilot-team task log (median 6 minutes
before, 4 during), and its speaker notes say other teams were not measured.
The question concerns claim fidelity, not design.

Quote: S-entry, "Entry is faster for everyone."
Source: the pilot log and S-entry's notes restrict evidence to the pilot team.
Mismatch: the visible message broadens the population beyond that evidence.
Consequence: an audience reading only message lines receives an unsupported
general claim. Recommend retaining the population limit visibly with the
message; do not write a substitute slide or claim a rendered fix.

Retain "Pilot entry time" as a topic title when a separate message carries
the conclusion. Its wording is not a defect. Without the log, factual support
is unverified; absent screenshots leave visual fit unverified. A preferred
text layout is optional preference, not proof that a slide is illegible.

QA: constrain findings to supplied slides and the requested textual question;
do not apply whole-deck progression requirements to an isolated wording review.

QA: the report distinguishes a textual sequence problem from an unverified
rendering concern. It does not contain a replacement deck, change slide IDs
or claim that the analysis produced or inspected a finished presentation.
