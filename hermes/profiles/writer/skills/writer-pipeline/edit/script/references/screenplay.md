# Editing screenplays

Preserve the approved scene IDs, cast, voice and story constraints while
applying named changes. Separate changed dialogue from changed action so the
consumer can identify the scope; a tone edit does not authorize a new motive.

Check entrances, knowledge, props and consequences around the edited scenes.
Mark removed IDs in the structural record without recycling them. Conflicts
with required lines or continuity go back to the requester, not into invented
backstory that makes the edit appear to work.

QA: read the full revision against the original, including untouched fields.
The script remains actor/producer-readable, but staging, acting and performed
duration have not been verified or changed by a wording edit.

## Craft Decisions

Preserve screenplay form and distinct SUPPLIED voice rather than smoothing
every character into the same grammar. Do not invent identity, accent or motive.
If action contains unfilmable commentary, replace it only when that field is
editable and the approved fiction supplies the observable action to express it.
If dialogue merely explains what both characters know, consider a scoped cut;
do not erase intentional evasion, hesitation or subtext. A no-op is legitimate.

## Worked Example (Fictional, MATERIAL-COMPLETE)

Supplied material: SC10, INT. HALL - DAY. Ada wants Ben to stay without asking;
she holds the door open and Ben pauses at it. Ada uses short questions, Ben
full polite sentences. Both know Ben is leaving. Only Ada's dialogue is editable;
the action, scene heading and Ben's "Yes, I should go." are protected.
Creative-fiction allowance: Ada's line within that intent, no new biography.
Original Ada line: "As you know, you are leaving. Are you leaving already?"
Revised scene, retaining the supplied form:
```text
SC10 - INT. HALL - DAY
Ada holds the door open. Ben pauses at it.
ADA
Already?
BEN
Yes, I should go.
```
Rationale: removing shared-knowledge exposition lets the question and held
door carry Ada's supplied intent; Ben's contrasting voice is not standardized.
Retain: the pause and question support subtext; keep existing lines that do too.
Counterexample: "Ada remembers her father's departure" invents unfilmable backstory.
QA: compare the changed line with the intent and quote all untouched fields;
approved fiction is not a fabricated real quote. Acting/duration stay unverified.
