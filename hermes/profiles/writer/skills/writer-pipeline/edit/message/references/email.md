# Editing an email

Preserve the reply's actual accept/decline/defer stance, conditions, dates and
commitments unless a change is explicitly requested. Do not turn a refusal
into a delay, or add "next time" to make a refusal sound warmer.

Keep quoted source, subject, body and signature boundaries intact. An optional
signature is not permission to invent a person's role or contact details.
Changing tone need not add an apology, reason or promise to follow up.

## Craft decisions

Use authorized changes to clarify the subject's purpose or bring the actual
sender stance forward; do not rebuild every email into the same sequence.
If structure is editable, put only necessary context after the answer and a
specific requested reply last, if any. A wording-only edit preserves field order.
Protect intent when trimming courtesy: a refusal remains a refusal, not a delay.
If the source already communicates the decision clearly, a no-op is valid.
Do not repair a perceived lack of warmth with an invented excuse or next-time promise.

## Worked example

Fictional material: subject 「勉強会への参加について」 is protected. The body is
「今回の勉強会につきましては、参加を辞退させていただきたく存じます。」
The request authorizes shortening the body while keeping a polite, definite
refusal. No reason, apology, future commitment or requested reply is supplied.
Revision body: 「今回の勉強会への参加は辞退いたします。」
Why: the revision removes the extended courtesy construction without changing
the sender's decision. The subject is returned unchanged in the complete draft.
No missing context needs to be invented to justify the refusal.

Retain: if the body already reads 「今回の勉強会への参加は辞退いたします。」,
return a no-op rather than a synonym swap. Counterexample: 「今回は難しいですが、
次回は参加します。」 adds a promise that the authorized edit does not permit.

QA evidence: quote the changed body alongside the request, compare the stance
before/after, and confirm the protected subject and untouched fields. A no-op
report states why the existing wording meets the request without new commitments.

QA: each change matches the requested fields/scope; protected intent remains
the same. Review notes are not part of the sendable body. A revised draft is
not an edit to a previously sent email and needs renewed approval if changed.
