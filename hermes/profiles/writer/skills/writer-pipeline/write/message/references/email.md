# Email draft

Use the actual recipient relationship, purpose and relevant thread context.
A reply must express the sender's chosen stance, not infer acceptance from
a request to sound friendly. Ask only when the missing stance changes the
reply. Names, signatures, dates and contact details come from supplied facts.

Separate the subject and body when a subject is requested or needed for the
new email. Answer the relevant question before adding necessary context or
a known next action. Do not paste the whole earlier thread, require ceremonial
greetings, or add promises, apologies and excuses to soften a refusal.

For example, with an explicit decision to decline an invitation and no reason
or future commitment supplied, a concise refusal is sufficient. Do not add
"next time" as a promise. Promotional mail belongs to Copy, not this channel.

## Craft decisions

Make the subject identify the purpose, then let the opening state the actual
sender stance. Add only context needed to understand that position. If a reply
is requested, specify what answer is needed and include only supplied timing;
otherwise stop without manufacturing a question. A refusal needs no excuse.
If the thread already identifies the invitation, avoid retelling its history.
Use a greeting only when the relationship or requested format benefits from it,
not as a condition of a complete message. Politeness must preserve the decision.

## Worked example

Fictional material: an organizer invited the sender to the 10月8日の勉強会.
The sender declines, wants a polite concise reply with a subject, and supplies
no reason, apology, future commitment, name or signature. No reply is requested.
Draft subject: 「10月8日の勉強会への参加について」
Draft body: 「10月8日の勉強会への参加は辞退いたします。」
Why: the subject identifies the invitation and the body gives the decided
answer. There is no filler context because none is needed to interpret it.
The restrained ending neither reopens the decision nor creates a follow-up.

Retain: a direct refusal already clear in its thread can remain this short.
Counterexample: 「予定が合わないため、次回はぜひ参加します。」 invents both
an excuse and a next-time promise; it is not merely a warmer version.

QA evidence: compare the subject's event/date and the body's refusal with the
brief; quote any requested reply to show that it asks only the authorized thing.
If no reply is needed, record that purpose match, not a missing-action defect.
