# Screenplay

Use the supplied scene format, cast, setting and story scope. Identify scenes
and speakers consistently; separate action and delivery instructions from exact
dialogue. Fictional invention must stay within the approved character/world
freedom and must not become invented quotes or experiences of real people.

Connect entrances, actions, knowledge and consequences across scenes without
imposing a fixed act count or ending. Keep actor-readable directions distinct
from the lines intended to be spoken. Do not select voices or perform the work.

QA: scene IDs/order, speaker attribution and story continuity are clear; required
lines and constraints survive. Page counts or reading estimates do not certify
performed duration, emotional effect, staging feasibility or a finished film.

## Craft Decisions

Translate an approved intent into observable action or a choice in dialogue;
do not replace it with unfilmable commentary about what the audience must feel.
When subtext matters, let a supplied gesture and the line differ in what they
reveal. Do not invent a hidden motive, identity or biography to create depth.
Use distinct SUPPLIED voice: vocabulary and grammar, not invented accents.
If a line only tells another character facts both already know, consider the
approved action that carries that information instead; keep screenplay form.

## Worked Example (Fictional, MATERIAL-COMPLETE)

Supplied material: scene SC10, INT. HALL - DAY; Ada wants Ben to stay but will
not ask directly. Ada holds the door open; Ben pauses at it. Ada speaks in
short questions, Ben in full, polite sentences. Both know he is leaving.
Creative-fiction allowance: dialogue for this intent and these actions only.
Draft in the supplied screenplay form:
```text
SC10 - INT. HALL - DAY
Ada holds the door open. Ben pauses at it.
ADA
Already?
BEN
Yes, I should go.
```
Rationale: Ada's question pressures the departure without stating her supplied
intent as exposition. Ben's complete sentence preserves the voice contrast.
Retain: the held door and pause carry subtext; do not replace them with an
explanation that Ada feels abandoned. No childhood or relationship is supplied.
Counterexample: "As you know, you are leaving" is exposition-as-dialogue here.
QA: compare action and speech with supplied intent/cast/voice, not real events.
Acting, emotional effect and duration remain unverified without performance.
