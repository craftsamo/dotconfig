# Landing-page copy

Start from the approved message and audience, not an invented positioning
exercise. Organize the supplied benefit, supporting evidence, offer conditions
and requested action so the reader can connect them. Choose the order for this
brief rather than forcing every page through an agitation or scarcity formula.

Keep qualifications near the claim they limit. A headline promising an outcome
cannot be repaired by a contradictory footnote. Name the actual eligible group,
price or trial condition when the offer requires it; missing material goes back
to the requester instead of becoming plausible fine print.

Supply text fields in the producer's requested format. Distinguish headings,
body and action labels from layout notes. Missing visual assets are dependencies,
not permission to make screenshots, invent customer logos or generate proof.

## Craft decisions

Connect the audience need to an approved benefit, then its substantiation and
adjacent terms. Use this as a reasoning path, not a fixed section formula.
If readers need to decide eligibility first, bring that condition forward.
If an approved benefit is a capability, explain its concrete use without turning
it into measured savings. Missing optional proof does not justify testimonials.
Choose a purpose-dependent CTA only when the brief calls for action and supplies
its target; an informational page need not manufacture urgency or scarcity.

## Worked example

Fictional material: an informational page addresses teams comparing a week's
reservations. Approved product facts: the Team plan displays one week's
reservations on one screen; the supplied feature sheet substantiates that
capability. It is available only on Team. The purpose requires no CTA or price.
Draft heading: 「1週間の予約をひとつの画面で確認」
Draft body: 「Team プランでは、1週間の予約をまとめて表示できます。」
Why: the heading answers the audience need with the approved capability;
the adjacent body keeps plan eligibility attached. The feature sheet supports
this display benefit, not a promise of time saved or increased revenue.

Retain: a concise benefit with its qualifying term needs no agitation section.
Counterexample: 「すべてのチームの作業時間を半減。今だけ無料。」 invents a result,
eligibility and scarcity rather than supplying missing substantiation.

QA evidence: connect each claim to its source passage and quote its adjacent
terms; compare any CTA with the released purpose and supplied target. Explain
why no CTA is appropriate when the page is informational, not an incomplete pitch.
