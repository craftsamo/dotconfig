# Meeting record

Use the meeting notes or transcript and any supplied metadata. Identify
decisions, proposals, action items and unresolved discussion without converting
one into another. Use the requested level of discussion detail; an attributed
quotation and an editorial summary are different representations.

Group by the actual agenda or outcome. Make decisions and actions findable,
but do not force empty sections, a conclusion-bearing heading for every
topic, or a paragraph where a short list is useful.

Only name an owner, deadline, speaker or agreement when the record supports
it. An absent deadline means "not recorded"; it does not prove the meeting
decided to leave it open. Preserve an explicitly undecided status as such.
If notes conflict or speaker identity is uncertain, retain that uncertainty
or request clarification. Do not assign an owner to make an action usable.

QA: each reported decision/action traces to the material, qualifications and
disagreements survive, and discussion is not silently promoted to agreement.
Quoted wording retains its meaning and speaker's stance. A concise record
must not erase an important condition on the apparent decision.
