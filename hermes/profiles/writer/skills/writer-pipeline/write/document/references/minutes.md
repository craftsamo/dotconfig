# Meeting record

Use the meeting notes or transcript and any supplied metadata. Identify
decisions, proposals, action items and unresolved discussion without converting
one into another. Use the requested level of discussion detail; an attributed
quotation and an editorial summary are different representations.

Group by the actual agenda or outcome. Make decisions and actions findable,
but do not force empty sections, a conclusion-bearing heading for every
topic, or a paragraph where a short list is useful.

Only name an owner, deadline, speaker or agreement when the record supports
it. An absent deadline means "not recorded"; it does not prove the meeting
decided to leave it open. Preserve an explicitly undecided status as such.
If notes conflict or speaker identity is uncertain, retain that uncertainty
or request clarification. Do not assign an owner to make an action usable.

## Expose outcomes without completing the record

Separate supported decisions and actions from proposals and questions so a
reader can find outcomes without replaying the discussion. Keep enough of the
reasoning to explain a condition or disagreement; do not allocate equal space
to every remark. Agenda order or an outcome summary can both work, depending
on the requested use, without mandatory empty sections.

Express an action as owner, action and deadline only to the extent recorded.
A missing component remains "not recorded," not "to be decided next time"
unless the record says so. Quote consequential ambiguous wording and identify
its uncertain referent or speaker instead of resolving it editorially.
An absent response cannot supply assent, even when agreement seems likely.

## Worked example and retain condition

Fictional teaching material: the chair says, "Keep the current alert threshold
until we review the trial." Maya says, "I'll send the trial log by Friday."
Another note reads "Let's change it later?" without an identified speaker.

An outcome summary can read: "Decision: retain the current alert threshold
until trial review. Action: Maya to send the trial log by Friday.
Open discussion: 'Let's change it later?' has no identified speaker or clear
referent in the notes; no threshold change is recorded as agreed."
This preserves the actual condition and commitment while keeping the question
out of the decisions. Do not convert Friday to a calendar date without context.

Retain "Deadline not recorded" if another action has no deadline in the notes.
"The team agreed to leave the deadline open" would invent an agreement.
An attributed quotation can stay colloquial when exact wording matters;
polishing it into a stronger promise would destroy its evidentiary role.

QA: for a released agenda item, trace its outcome and any summary mention to
the same record; missing metadata elsewhere need not block that piece.

QA: each reported decision/action traces to the material, qualifications and
disagreements survive, and discussion is not silently promoted to agreement.
Quoted wording retains its meaning and speaker's stance. A concise record
must not erase an important condition on the apparent decision.
