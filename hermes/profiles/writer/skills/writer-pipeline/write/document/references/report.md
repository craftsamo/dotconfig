# Report

Use the supplied question, observation period, population, method, results and
known limitations. Match the scope to the kind of report: a short status
update does not need an invented research-method section.

Summarize the supported answer, then provide enough definitions and evidence
to interpret it. Keep factual observations, calculations, interpretation and
recommendations distinguishable. Put material limitations near the affected
claim, not only at the end. Compare periods or groups only under compatible
definitions, or explain the difference.

Do not turn correlation into causation or assume every non-retained member
of a population belongs to one particular exit category. Recommendations
are included when requested and supported, not added to satisfy a "So What"
template. Missing data should limit the conclusion rather than invite one.

## Build the answer

For a decision report, lead with the supported answer and the decision it can
inform. For a descriptive status note, lead with the observed change; no
recommendation is needed. Follow with the comparison baseline, evidence and
limitations needed to interpret that answer. Use a table when readers must
compare the same measures across groups; explain causes in prose only when
the evidence supports them. A heading must retain a provisional conclusion's
uncertainty rather than turn it into a result.

Keep the denominator, period and unit beside the first important number.
Distinguish a percentage-point change from a relative percentage change.
Introduce an interpretation as an interpretation and name the observation
supporting it. If the material cannot answer the question, identify that gap
instead of padding the report with general background or an invented example.

## Worked example and counterexample

Fictional teaching material: 30 of 50 respondents report satisfaction; the
number of all customers and the response rate are unknown.
"60% of customers are satisfied" expands the population without evidence.
"30 of the 50 respondents (60%) reported satisfaction; the response rate is
unknown" preserves what the material establishes. This supports a statement
about respondents, not a causal claim or a recommendation to expand service.

"Response time fell from 10 ms to 8 ms in the supplied benchmark" can remain
as written when its test conditions are already clear. Do not replace a
specific observation with "performance improved significantly" or add a
research-method section to a short status note merely for symmetry.

QA: the summary and body agree, numerical definitions and qualifiers survive,
and sources support the stated interpretation. Supplied results are not an
independently reproduced experiment or an externally verified conclusion.
