# Report

Use the supplied question, observation period, population, method, results and
known limitations. Match the scope to the kind of report: a short status
update does not need an invented research-method section.

Summarize the supported answer, then provide enough definitions and evidence
to interpret it. Keep factual observations, calculations, interpretation and
recommendations distinguishable. Put material limitations near the affected
claim, not only at the end. Compare periods or groups only under compatible
definitions, or explain the difference.

Do not turn correlation into causation or assume every non-retained member
of a population belongs to one particular exit category. Recommendations
are included when requested and supported, not added to satisfy a "So What"
template. Missing data should limit the conclusion rather than invite one.

QA: the summary and body agree, numerical definitions and qualifiers survive,
and sources support the stated interpretation. Supplied results are not an
independently reproduced experiment or an externally verified conclusion.
