# Slide outline

This format delivers text for a presentation, not a rendered deck. Use the
audience, purpose, approved claims, available evidence and any slide/length
constraints. Preserve the producer's required fields when provided.

Give each slide a stable ID and a role in the sequence. Separate visible
title/body text from speaker notes and production requests. Put the point
and its supporting evidence together when that is the slide's purpose;
an agenda or transition slide need not pretend to state a new conclusion.

Use as many bullets or sections as the actual content needs within the brief,
not a fixed three-part rule. Do not add statistics, imagery descriptions or
a closing CTA merely to fill slots. Name missing required graphics as work
for the responsible producer rather than a completed visual.

## Carry the argument in message lines

Distinguish the topic (what this slide covers), message line (what the audience
should understand), evidence (what supports it) and speaker notes (delivery
context). Map those roles into supplied producer fields rather than inventing
a new schema. A title may already carry the message; duplicating it is not
required. These roles guide authorship, not a fixed slide template.

For argument-bearing slides, the message lines in sequence should convey the
qualified argument without depending on hidden notes. Put a condition that
limits a claim in visible text. Use evidence for support, not paraphrases of
the message; keep explanations needed only by the speaker in notes. A topic
label remains appropriate for navigation or an agenda. Do not force a fixed
slide or bullet count, a new conclusion, or an action into every role.

## Worked example and retain condition

Fictional teaching material: a task log for a pilot team reports median
entry time falling from 6 to 4 minutes; it does not measure other teams.
The requester wants a status outline, not an expansion recommendation.

S-entry topic: "Pilot entry time."
Message line: "Median entry time fell in the pilot team."
Evidence: "Supplied task log: 6 minutes before, 4 minutes during the pilot."
Speaker notes: "Explain that other teams were not measured."
S-scope message line: "Effects outside the pilot team remain unknown."
Together the lines communicate the result and boundary. The visible pilot
qualification matters even if the speaker never reads the notes aloud.

Retain "Agenda" as a navigation title. "Entry is faster for everyone" would
overstate the evidence; hiding "pilot only" in notes would not repair it.
Do not invent an expansion request to make this status outline end in a CTA.

QA: read the released message-line sequence for qualified continuity and
check evidence placement; an isolated slide needs only its relevant context.

QA: the intended progression is coherent, visible text and notes are distinct,
and evidence supports claims. Source text alone cannot prove legibility,
layout fit, speaking duration or the quality of a presentation file.
