# Experience Report

Use the supplied participant's account or records. Separate what happened,
what the participant thought at the time and what they conclude now.
Do not create first-person experiences, emotions, dialogue or chronology
to make the narrative more engaging.

Select the events that explain the point rather than preserving every detail.
Keep important uncertainty and contradictions visible; ask if resolving them
would change the story. A requested voice sample is not autobiographical data.

QA: every presented experience is grounded or explicitly hypothetical, and
the reported lesson does not claim universal results from one account.

## Build from the Account

If a supplied event explains a change in the participant's understanding,
give that event room: what happened, what was observed, then the participant's
interpretation. Attribute retrospective judgment instead of projecting it
back into the event. A narrative need not be conclusion-first when discovery
or an unresolved ending is part of the requested reading experience.

Locally authored example (illustrative supplied-account scenario):
Record: "I missed the turn. I went back to the station map. I think I relied
too much on remembering the route."
Draft: "I missed the turn and went back to the station map. Looking back,
I think I relied too much on remembering the route."
Reason: the small event supports the reflection without inventing panic,
weather, dialogue or a warm personal confession. The tentative explanation
remains tentative; it does not become "The signs were inadequate."

Retain: a pause, colloquial quotation or open ending that carries the supplied
voice. Do not add a universal lesson such as "Getting lost teaches us to live"
merely to close the piece. A practical incident report may instead lead with
its known outcome; choose by purpose, not by an essay stereotype.
Compress incidental travel details only when they do not explain the event.

QA evidence: connect each event and attributed feeling to the supplied account;
quote the observation and interpretation separately. Identify where chronology
or causality is unknown, and check that the ending does not strengthen either.
Explain retained narrative pacing through its role, not an authorship judgment.
