# Analyzing an email

Identify what the text explicitly asks, accepts, declines, promises or leaves
open. Use the supplied thread context and quote the passages supporting a
reading. A polite formula is not proof of enthusiasm, intent to comply or blame.

Compare tone or clarity against the stated recipient/purpose when relevant;
do not require private relationship records for a bounded wording question.
Distinguish likely interpretations from facts about the sender or recipient.

## Craft decisions

Trace the subject's purpose to the actual sender stance in the opening, then
inspect whether context is necessary and any requested reply is specific.
If reviewing fit, quote the relevant words, compare supplied intent/evidence,
identify the mismatch and explain its possible consequence. Do not turn a
polite refusal into evidence of an excuse, hidden enthusiasm or a future promise.
If no reply is needed, its absence is not a defect. Keep conclusions conditional
on the available thread; analysis supplies no replacement email or new greeting.

## Worked example

Fictional material: subject 「勉強会への参加について」; body 「今回は参加を辞退します。
次回は必ず参加します。」 The supplied intent is a definite refusal with no future
commitment. The requester asks whether this draft preserves that stance.
Finding: quote 「次回は必ず参加します」 as the unsupported commitment; the brief
authorizes declining this invitation but supplies no next-time promise.
Consequence: the recipient could reasonably expect future attendance, so this
clause changes the commitment rather than merely softening the refusal.
Why: the finding joins exact wording to the supplied criterion and explains
the mismatch without asserting the recipient's actual feelings or drafting a reply.

Retain: 「今回は参加を辞退します。」 matches the supplied stance; identify that
supported passage rather than inventing an apology requirement.
Counterexample: calling the sender evasive from a short refusal invents motive;
describing a possible reading must not become a psychological verdict.

QA evidence: retain the minimal quote, the compared stance and the bounded
consequence in the report. If the intended stance is unavailable, describe the
expressed commitment without certifying a mismatch against an imaginary brief.
