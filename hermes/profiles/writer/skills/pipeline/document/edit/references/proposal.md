# Editing a proposal

Preserve the question to be decided, the requester's actual position, option
boundaries and known trade-offs. An open comparison must not become a chosen
plan just because a conclusion-first style was requested.

Keep estimates, assumptions and commitments distinct. New rankings, budgets,
deadlines or implementation stages need authorization and supporting material.
Use the same comparison criteria after reorganizing alternatives.

## Clarify the choice within its authority

Repair a buried decision sought only within the released wording or structure
scope. When reorganizing alternatives, compare them on the same axes and keep
the reasons, trade-offs and conditions attached. Do not promote a supplied
provisional stance into a selected plan. These guides do not require adding
a recommendation to an intentionally open discussion memo.

Leave untouched sections and protected content unchanged. An unsupported
budget, ranking or preference is a conflict to flag, not a fact to complete.
If the existing comparison is accurate and usable, a no-op is legitimate.
Moving evidence must not separate the condition that limits the conclusion.

## Worked example and retain condition

Fictional teaching material: the request permits clarifying only the opening
decision sentence. The requester leans toward recorded training if delayed
answers are acceptable. The comparison of live and recorded formats on access
and feedback, and its unknown-cost note, are protected.

Before: "Choose recordings for the pilot if that is acceptable."
After: "Decide whether to pilot recordings, the requester's provisional
preference if delayed answers are acceptable."
The referent and decision status come from the supplied brief, not the
editor's opinion. The same-axis comparison and unknown costs stay unchanged;
the correction neither supplies approval nor declares recordings the winner.

Retain "The choice remains open pending feedback requirements" when that
is the requester's position. Recasting it as "Approve recordings now" is
not a stronger introduction but a different decision. A wording-only scope
cannot authorize a new budget or silently repair protected claim conflicts.

QA: compare changed decision wording with the supplied stance and conditions;
inspect affected comparisons without expanding into a new proposal.

QA: the proposal asks for the same decision within the released edit scope.
Changes to the conclusion match an explicit request; stylistic confidence
does not create approval, a guarantee or an unrecorded preference.
